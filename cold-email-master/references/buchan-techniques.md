## 1. The model: the email's job is small

- "The job of the cold email is to sell the idea that a call or a meeting with you is not a terrible idea." Not the whole offer. [L04]
- Impact, then communicate, then persuade. Most cold emails fail by going straight to persuasion.
- **Plain-voice application:** the house four variables already do this. Relevance and pain are the impact. One plain sentence of offer. A small ask.

## 2. The Way In: answer the stranger question honestly

- Every cold email has an elephant: "Who are you, and what are you doing in my inbox?" Buchan answers it before the pitch instead of pretending it isn't there. [SITE 031]
- **Plain-voice moves (pick at most one, inside the Relevance line or right after it):**
  - Name the honest source: "Found you through the {association} directory." / "Your name came up in three different reviews for the same thing."
  - Admit it's cold, briefly: "We haven't met, so I'll keep this short."
  - Lead with the reason: "Writing because {signal}."
- Not every email needs it. The house relevance line often answers it implicitly. Use it where the detail alone would feel like surveillance.

## 3. Disarming honesty (the Warren Buffett technique)

- Open with the most honest, least flattering true thing, and everything after gains credibility. [INT reddit] "If a joke doesn't fit, be more honest than is expected." [INT yt-drunk-cold-email]
- **Plain-voice moves:**
  - Say what it isn't: "This isn't a sales call dressed up as a feature." (The Real Experts terms line is this move.)
  - Admit the limit: "It won't suit everyone. It suits owners who {condition}."
  - Admit the pitch: "Yes, this is a pitch. A short one."
- One honest admission per email, not several.

## 4. "I help X without Y"

- The offer in one line, with the friction removed in the second half. [Magic email annotation]
- **Plain-voice form:** "I help {who} {get outcome} without {the thing they hate}." / "We run {thing} so you don't have to."
- Pairs with the house done-for-you and no-risk signature lines. Keep the "without" half to one item; Buchan's triple is a humour device and is out.

## 5. Escape hatches (an easy way out in every ask)

- Buchan's single most transferable move: every ask carries a way out, so saying no costs nothing, and saying yes feels safe. "Just gives them an easy way out." [Magic email annotation]
- **Plain-voice bank (rotate, never repeat within a lead):**
  - "If it's not for you, no problem."
  - "Totally fine if the answer is no."
  - "Even a 'not for me' helps, so I stop guessing."
  - "If the timing's wrong, say so and I'll leave it."
  - "Yours either way." (house)
- Anticipate negative replies: a calm, pre-emptive out stops many of them. [Magic email annotation: "Anticipate negative replies"]

## 6. Frame the ask as their gain

- "Ideas you're free to steal." The meeting should feel useful and safe. [8-Part PDF]
- **Plain-voice form:** the house walkthrough close is this move. "Reply and I'll send {reward}. Yours either way, run it with us or don't."

## 7. Specific, odd numbers look real

- "A quick 7-minute chat" instead of 15 or 30, because an odd number looks "just that little bit different". [Magic email annotation]
- **House application:** odd, specific times in the booking cadence ("10:15 or 2:40"). The "15 minutes" in the walkthrough close stays the house default; a "7 minutes" or "12 minutes" variant is a valid A/B test.

## 8. Write to the person, not the title

- "Most people are people first, professionals second." Respect them; don't put them on a pedestal. [Voice rules]
- Understated but ambitious. Meek but direct. No hype. No "faux cocky". No ego.
- Never mock the prospect. Never lecture them about their own business.
- Don't tame it into corporate speak for corporates. Plain, informal text filled HP's events after the designed invite drew no one. [INT eofire]

## 9. Personalisation that's honest

- Three honest types: **Opportunity** (an improvement you spotted), **Commonality** (real shared ground), **Topicality** (news, a launch, a post they wrote). [NL 2023-01-09]
- Never fake common ground. "You like kayaking? I went kayaking once!" is the anti-example. [SITE 104]
- One or two personal facts at most; stacking reads as surveillance. [Applied guardrail] (House rule is stricter: one.)
- Personalisation level matches account value: minimal for thousands, one true line for hundreds, fully one-to-one for top accounts. The house researches every lead to the "one true line" level; the Spearhead tier is fully one-to-one.
- Trigger worth using: "changed jobs in the past 90 days" finds people open to new ideas. [cold-email.md]

## 10. Follow-up types, in plain voice

Buchan's follow-up map is the best taxonomy of *why* a follow-up gets answered. Use it to give each of the ten house follow-ups a distinct job. No jokes, no images.

| Type | Plain-voice shape | Fishes for |
|---|---|---|
| Forgot to mention | One new true detail or line the framework email didn't have, then the question | Reads like a personal note |
| Honest no-reply admission | "No reply yet, which usually means busy, not no." + the question | Any reply, including no |
| The useful one | One relevant, verified fact or observation about their world + why it matters to them + the question | Value |
| Yes or no both welcome | "A quick yes or no is plenty, either one helps." | A decision |
| Three options (plain) | "Ignore this and I'll get the hint. Reply and I'll send {reward}. Or call me on {number} if that's easier." | A reply as the easy middle option |
| Pure value, no ask | Send the useful thing, no question (use sparingly; house follow-ups normally end on a question, so this lives in Closer 2 territory) | Goodwill |
| Breakup | "I may have jumped the gun." "This is my last note." "If I've got it wrong, a one-line reply is all it takes." | The distracted reader's last chance |

- Every nudge adds value or a new reason. Never "You didn't reply to my email. Are you interested?" [SITE 115]
- Buchan's breakup is his plainest email: courtesy and a clean end. That matches the house Closer 2.

## 11. Reply model

- Five reactions: ignore everything (you can't win them all), amused but not now, meant to reply and got distracted (follow-ups exist for these), delighted, angry (the smallest group). [SITE 031]
- After a positive reply, be plain, enthusiastic and expert. [Tone guardrails]
- "Not now": ask to check back in 3, 6 or 9 months, and keep the date.
- Polite no: one-line apology, unsubscribe. Rude: unsubscribe, no reply.
- Diagnose the mix, not one message. Only if replies are all negative, look at data, copy, offer.
- Full triage in `reply-playbook.md`.

## 12. Opt-outs, done well

- Buchan includes an opt-out where the law requires it and writes it in his own voice. [Opt-out lines]
- **Plain-voice bank (rotate):**
  - "If this isn't for you, just say so and I'll stop."
  - "Not your thing? One word back and you won't hear from me again."
  - "If you'd rather I didn't write again, reply 'stop' and that's that."
- Never "silence means no". Never imply you'll keep emailing regardless.

## 13. Dream clients: do something special

- For the handful of accounts that matter most, make something just for them (a custom map, a sketch of their numbers, a printed one-pager, a handwritten letter), then follow up by email naming it: "Did a printed map of your town's referral partners land on your desk?" [cold-email.md, Props and dream clients]
- House: this is the Spearhead tier in `strategy-playbook.md` §5. No novelty props or joke enclosures; the special thing is genuinely useful.
- Direct-mail + email combo: the email a few days after the letter names the letter in the subject and covers "if it didn't arrive".

## 14. Data and volume notes from Buchan

- Mass mail goes to verified business addresses only. [cold-email.md]
- "Sending fewer emails with more personalisation, because then you get to pick who you work with." Volume scales inversely with price. [INT fearless-business]
- Re-email non-repliers after the sequence ends: "another email in a month or two". House default is 3 months with a new angle.
