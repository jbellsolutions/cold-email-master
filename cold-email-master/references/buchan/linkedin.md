# LinkedIn (runtime reference)

Templates are verbatim from the course. **Dating caveat:** the course is from about 2017. Treat UI steps, prices and daily limits as "as stated in the course", and check current LinkedIn behaviour. The copy principles are not dated.
**Recency rule:** where the 2021–2026 newsletters change a course figure, follow his latest stated practice and keep the course figure as history (knowledge/00 §0).

## Role and targeting

- **Three jobs:** prospect research (Sales Navigator), a conversation channel (invites and DMs), and reach (content to a network you've filled with ideal clients).
- **Search variables.**
  - Core: job title, location, company size, sector, plus keywords.
  - Trigger tabs: "Changed jobs in past 90 days" (his "game changer"; new starters "haven't got the inertia"; they "want to impress their bosses" [NL 2026-02-18]), "Mentioned in the news in past 30 days" (a ready-made personal line), "Posted on LinkedIn in past 30 days" (stack it on the others [NL 2026-02-18]). Post search: "looking for [what you do here]", sorted by latest [NL 2023-02-03 free-tip].
- **Boolean.** "A huge part of what you ARE looking for is what you are NOT looking for." Examples:
  - `CEO NOT Assistant NOT PA`
  - `("Founder" OR "CEO" OR "Managing Director") NOT "Non Executive" NOT "Board Member"`
  - Stack keywords: `Marketing AND Innovation AND Disruptive`.
- **The search words become copy.** Trait keywords ("disruptive", "ambitious", "creative thinker") find people who self-identify with them, so "craftily use this when cold messaging them". "You can add these search terms to your messaging copy!"

## Profile (when the user asks for profile copy)

- **Order of work:** one-line pitch → good photo and cover → bio ("the biggest piece of work").
- **Headline:** a "very succinct one-line pitch of who you can help and what you do to help them". Example: "I help founders stand out, book more sales calls and build their influence online." Optional: an emoji after the name (his 🏅) to stand out in comments and search.
- **Photo.** A good photo is enough. The wacky option, which he recommends highly: a Fiverr photoshop of you in a sector-relevant absurd scene (his: rescuing a baby and kittens from a burning building). People "reference the silly picture", and it pairs with the cutting-insult invite. The self-selection is deliberate: "not an unfortunate side effect ... a deliberate feature" [INT eofire 14:41].
- **Banner:** social proof (him speaking) plus a curiosity hook (a screenshot of a cold email).
- **About: Identity → Struggle → Discovery → Surprise → link → CTA.**
  - Informal, no jargon, and "Refreshing honesty is welcome".
  - It's less ridiculous than the cold copy, but "not all formal and stuffy".
  - Include how you help: "I can get the attention of busy people. I help ambitious businesses stand out, book sales calls and build their influence online."
  - Alternative opener: "Greetings / I have a weird skill / I can write emails and letters that open opportunities."
- **Featured ("Media" in 2017):** two links only, your current goal and your best proof.
- **Recommendations:** give them, politely ask, and they'll come back. Genuine peers only ("it has to be genuine").
- **Honest education** ("Pretty Mediocre GCSEs") gets mentioned in messages. Enter every skill.
- **Thin experience or a day job:** name it honestly and frame it as enthusiasm. "Test it."

## Invite rules (apply to every invite)

- **Character limit.** Buchan gives no number; LinkedHelper enforced it. *Platform fact, not Buchan:* a connection note is limited to 300 characters, and lower on some free accounts. Check the current limit, and always show the count.
- **Counts as written** (with `{firstname}` and line breaks): at-scale 136 · one/two punch 228 · triple-reverse opener 118. A long first name adds characters.
- **Use only `{firstname}`.** Company and position tags "don't work properly ... it's obvious that it's just been pasted in".
- **Volume (his latest advice; the skill only drafts).** Course: LinkedHelper, about 150 actions per 24h in total [L05 29:58]. 2023: "Connect with 50 people per day without using automation and without personalising every connection request" [NL 2023-07-26 whats-in-linkedin]. 2026: "You don't need to personalise these. You can send 100 a week with a free account. This increases to up to 200 a week with LinkedIn Sales Navigator." [NL 2026-02-17]. **Default to the weekly figures.** LinkedHelper is optional: he hasn't used it "in ages" but recommends it again [NL 2026-02-05]. "Don't go overboard ... you can get in trouble with LinkedIn." Respect the user's own tool settings.

## At scale

**Network building: "not the generic invite"** [PDF LI-Templates p2]
> Hey hey {firstname},
> I really wanted to connect. As such, I've decided not to send you the generic LinkedIn invite!
> Fingers crossed,
> Jon

- "Even with a generic invite, a lot of people will accept. Sending something a little more interesting and human works well at scale."
- It names the convention it breaks (meta-honesty), then closes with a hopeful, low-pressure "Fingers crossed".
- "Personalised invites are best to key prospects you REALLY want to work with ... when inviting at scale, this isn't possible. This is the next best thing."

**Lead gen: the "One/Two Punch", part 1** [PDF LI-Templates p4]. Part 2 is under Post-accept.
> Hey hey {firstname},
> Nice to 'meet' you. I have a weird skill that means I can book meetings with just about anybody.
> If you're at all curious, click 'accept'. :)
> If not, send me your most cutting insult. :(
> Fingers crossed,
> Jon

- **The offer line: "Replace this with your offering".** The formula is "I have a weird skill that means I can [real outcome]", and it must "engender curiosity".
- **The cutting-insult line** "adds humour to the call to action, gives them a way out, and sets up the follow up message". "Most people [accept] ... Nobody really does [insult]."
- **The mechanics:** the CTA *is* the accept button; the escape hatch is a joke, so declining costs nothing; `:)` and `:(` keep it light.

**Alternative opener he has used for invites** (triple reverse; on LinkedIn, swap "email" for "invite". That swap is our adaptation, not his wording):
- "I'd like to introduce myself in a way that shows I'm interesting, witty and clever. Alas, I wrote this email instead." He says: "I've used this for LinkedIn invites, for emails, for all sorts of things" [L03 7:23].

## Key prospect

- **There's no verbatim Buchan key-prospect invite in the sources.** Draft from the method below and label it "adapted from Buchan's method". Never present it as his template.
- **Method:**
  1. Save them as a **Lead** in Sales Navigator, so their posts show in your feed.
  2. **Like and comment over time** (e.g. "nice work" on their video). This "gently builds rapport" and gives you name recognition.
  3. **Then invite (or email), referencing their activity, article or news mention.** It's "really worth the effort when you've got those key prospects".
- **Raw material:** their background, what recommendations say about them, shared interests (genuine only), and the "mentioned in the news" tab.
- **Personalisation tiers** (from the cold-email lesson). One true line beats none; a fully one-to-one message gets "the greatest response but is also the most work", so save it for big accounts.
- **Never fake an interest.** Use their article instead.
- **Sold but absent from `sources/`. Do NOT invent copy and attribute it to Buchan:**
  - profile/recommendation-based invite + follow-up
  - comment-then-DM
  - "recently posted" messages
  - "LinkedIn Virgin" (Member Since filter)
  - "Right Time" (90-day job change)
  - same-first-name, rival-team and common-interest pitches
  - birthday, new job, work anniversary, news-feature, profile-viewer, new-follower, "looking for suppliers" and "I love your content" messages
  - the "Invite / Follow Up Message Combo"
  - the "List Building Hack"

## Post-accept

**One/Two Punch, part 2: "thanks for not insulting me"** [PDF LI-Templates p5]
> Hello there {firstname},
> Thanks for not sending me a cutting insult!
> If you're at all curious about the ideas I mentioned, let me know and I'll spill the beans.
> I say 'ideas'… It is of course, a thinly veiled sales pitch. However, I often get too enthusiastic and give away great ideas. You should take advantage of this!
> Have an absolutely wonderful day,
> Jon

- **"Immediately references the humour in the invite copy".** The invite sits in the thread, so the callback lands.
- **"Disarming honesty":** it calls the pitch "a thinly veiled sales pitch".
- **"Make it look like they stand to gain, rather than being hard-sold too".**
- **DM tone (newsletters):** "charming, humorous, and not-at-all entitled or boring", with some "risk reversal" (a free audit or brainstorm "you can deliver in 20 minutes") [NL 2026-02-17]. No "obvious subterfuge" small talk ("How is business going for you today?") [NL 2023-02-06].
- **Timing: send it by hand, "immediately or as soon as you can" after they accept.** Log in "a few times a day". **Don't automate this message.** Mark drafts "send manually ASAP".
- It's a DM, so the 300-character invite limit doesn't apply. His DMs run 4–8 lines in practice. "keep LinkedIn messages shorter than emails" is **(unverified — legacy)** as a rule.

## Follow-ups

**Sector-specific (to 1st connections)** [PDF LI-Templates p3]
> Thanks for connecting with me, {firstname}. I hope you don't regret this as I send you this very brief sales pitch!
>  - My name is {name} and I'm a {what you do}
>  - My passion is {sector}… and I'd love to work with more clients in this sector
>  - This message is my attempt at getting your attention!
>
> I'd love to give you some ideas you're free to steal.
> Let me know if you'd like to chat.
> Have a great day,
> {name}

- **"Make light of the sudden pitch".** The first line is "your super honest first line that will get a smile".
- **"Show you love the sector they are in".** "People like to work with enthusiastic people."
- **"Ideas you're free to steal"** means "less objections for getting them on a call". The CTA is soft.

**Keyword-specific** [PDF L05 p18]. It's in the lesson deck, not the templates PDF.
> Thanks for connecting with me, {firstname}. I hope you don't regret this as I send you this very brief sales pitch!
>  - My name is {name} and I'm a {what you do}
>  - I'm looking to work with ambitious and innovative founders - and I think you fit that bill!
>  - This message is my attempt at getting your attention!
>
> I'd love to give you some ideas you're free to steal.
> Let me know if you'd like to chat.
> Have a great day,
> Jon

- **"Use the keywords that are on their bio".** Swap in the exact words you searched on, so it "will resonate even more".

**Broadcasting these at scale (his practice; the skill only drafts)**
1. Search your 1st connections by keyword and title.
2. Collect them in LinkedHelper's "Message broadcast to 1st connections".
3. **Exclude existing clients**, and anyone you shouldn't message.
4. Send the sector or keyword template above, including the search keywords.

Broadcast supports `{company}` and `{position}`, but first-name-only is the safer inference.

**Cadence: what the sources do and don't say**
- **Stated:**
  - post-accept message ASAP;
  - broadcasts in batches, alternating with invite weeks, within LinkedIn's limits (course: ~150 actions/day [L05 29:58]; latest: 100–200 invites/week [NL 2026-02-17]).
- **Not stated anywhere:**
  - a LinkedIn nudge sequence;
  - "second follow-up after N days";
  - any LinkedIn follow-up copy beyond the templates above.
- **(unverified — legacy):** "follow-up within a day or two while the invite is fresh" (Buchan says "immediately or as soon as you can"), and "one light nudge later — never a pressure chase". If the user wants a nudge, write one in his voice and label it adapted.
- **The email cadence is email-only.** "1 follow-up email every 7 days for 7 weeks" (course) and "5 working days" (2026 [NL 2026-02-02]) are *email* rules; don't present either as a LinkedIn rule.

**Cross-channel: email reply → LinkedIn invite** [PDF t10 p9 / p4]. Send the booked-call version (113 characters) once they accept a call. Send the "not right now" version only *after* they answer your 3/6/9-month question (detail in knowledge/05).
> Thanks for replying to my email, {name}. I hope you don't mind, but would love to connect on here!
> Thank you,
> Jon

The "not right now" variant: "Hey {name}, thanks for replying to my email. I'd love to connect with you on LinkedIn, if you don't mind. Thank you, Jon." [PDF t10 p4].

## Content (posts)

- **Be interesting and tell stories.** "Don't pitch all the time!" If you pitch, "hide it with humour".
- **Formulas:**
  - speaking "the truth" / going against convention;
  - a succinct story (the drunk cold email);
  - making a point with humour (the mock "Direct Mail!" product launch).
- **"No walls of text!"** One line, then a space.
- **Put links in the first comment,** never the body ("LinkedIn restricts your reach"). End the post with "If you'd like to grab a copy, check the link in the comments."
- **Order of work:** connect with ideal clients "every day", then start posting [INT fearless-business 23:03].
- **Get early engagement:** friends, groups and pods. Most views come from 2nd+ degree connections.
- **Memes** make pitch posts "more palatable"; link in the comments [NL 2024-10-27 your-pitch-posts].
- **Voice notes:** sourced only as a pack item, "An amusing voicenote script for sending prospects on LinkedIn. The use of a voicenote is a pattern interrupt in itself" [NL 2023-08-15 whats-in-the]. The script isn't in the sources: any script is Applied.

## Not covered: don't attribute to Buchan

- LinkedIn video DMs.
- InMail.
- Enrichment tools or scrapers for research (dropped; his research is the profile, recommendations, the news tab and their posts).

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| A blank, generic invite | A short, human, slightly cheeky note |
| Company or position merge tags | `{firstname}` only |
| Automating the post-accept message | Send it by hand, ASAP |
| Going over LinkedIn's weekly invite limit | Stay within 100 (free) / 200 (Sales Navigator) a week [NL 2026-02-17]; course: ~150 actions/day [L05 29:58] |
| Disguised small-talk DM ("How is business going for you today?") | Say why you're there, charmingly [NL 2023-02-06] |
| A cold pitch alone to a dream prospect | Save as a lead and engage first, then reference their activity |
| Broadcasting to existing clients | Review the recipients and exclude them |
| Links in the post body; walls of text; constant pitching | First-comment links, short lines, stories, a pitch hidden in humour |
| A stuffy bio, or a Featured section crammed with links | An informal, honest ISDS bio with a CTA; two links |

## Applied guardrails (ours, from eval review)
- Never write a disclaimer or joke that contradicts the sender's offer or proof (e.g. "not here to talk you out of hiring SDRs" when the
  offer *replaces* SDRs). Genuine-only applies to throwaway lines too.
- Make the offer fit the segment. If most of the segment doesn't do the thing you fix, reframe the offer or tighten the segment.

## Drafting checklist

1. **Mode picked:** at scale (network, or lead-gen one/two punch), key prospect (warm up first), post-accept or follow-up.
2. **Invite:** within the platform note limit, with the character count shown, `{firstname}` as the only merge field, one hook, and a low-pressure, humorous way out.
3. **"Weird skill" line:** names a real, curiosity-inducing outcome from the sender's actual offer.
4. **Post-accept:** calls back to the invite joke, names the pitch honestly, frames it as their gain, and is labelled "send manually ASAP".
5. **Follow-up:** reuses the sector or the exact bio/search keywords, and ends with a soft "Let me know if you'd like to chat."
6. **Key prospect:** references genuine, specific activity (a post, an article, a news mention), is labelled adapted (no Buchan template exists), and never fakes an interest.
7. **No invented Buchan copy** for the sold-but-absent message types, and no LinkedIn cadence presented as his.
8. **Drafts only.** Nothing is sent or scheduled, and automation advice stays within the user's own tool limits.
