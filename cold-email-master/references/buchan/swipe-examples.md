# Swipe Examples (runtime reference)

The 18 strongest pieces of real Buchan outreach, verbatim, with one line on why each works and what to steal. Formula names refer to voice-and-humour.md; mechanics (cadence, letter design, subject rules) are in cold-email.md and linkedin.md. ## Cold email

### 1. The original drunk cold email ("Apologies in advance") · [RES slideshare] slides 11–21 · verbatim
```text
Subject: Apologies in advance :(
Greetings Susan,
You've never heard of me. (Hi, I'm Jon) I got your details from a list *gasp*. But hey - you're list worthy, that's gotta be something, right? :-)
OK. Let's get down to brass tacks. I'm emailing you because I run a digital marketing agency in Clapham, London, called Render Positive with my brother, Gary, and a team of 10.
I used to work at large digital agencies with big brand clients offering online marketing services (SEO, PPC, Social Media, Analytics, Content Creation and Bedroom Tidying).
For nearly 5 years, we've run our own agency and we're absolutely loving it. It's so good to not feel like we are ripping clients off, and we are doing the best work of our lives.
We recently got an office in Clapham North and I'm looking to win new clients over to our side and away from those evil (ok maybe not evil… but not as good) other agencies.
We have some big names on board - but being the ambitious bunch we are, we won't settle for anything other than total domination.
If you let me have a chat with you about your digital marketing needs, I will buy you coffee / lunch / tequila shots and promise to be somewhat entertaining. If you're lucky, I may even wear a top hat.
First off, I'd love to provide you with some ideas you are free to steal.
I have attached a picture of a ferret that has been dressed up. According to the internet, his name is Colin. I trust this will charm you into submission.
I await your profanity filled response.
Have an absolutely wonderful day,
Jon Buchan
```
- **Why:** answers "Who are you? And what are you doing in my inbox?" before any pitch [SITE 031]; it sold the call, not the agency. Result: meetings with "RedBull, Pepsi, Symantec, Hewlett-Packard, HSBC, Barclays…" [SITE 062]; "it was the ferret that did it ;-)". **Steal:** admit the awkward truth, turn it into a compliment ("list worthy"), end on a silly attachment + "I trust this will charm you into submission".
### 2. Aaron's "Magic Cold Email" · [PDF L04 p14] [PDF Magic-Cold-Email p1] · student example (Buchan's margin notes in brackets)
```text
Bonjour {{first name}},                                   [Unusual Greeting]
You've never heard of me. Hi, I'm Aaron. I got your email from top secret servers using a biorhythmic metaspyder
with a 100GB nutro-plasmic VPN that I….                    [Amusing honest opening line. How did you get their details?]
Ok, I got it from Clearbit.
Let me get right down to it…
I don't know if this is a problem you have, but I help software companies convert their list of non-paying, expired
trial users into paid users with ongoing email nurturing.  [Formula - I help [X] without [X]]
I'll write the emails, you send them to your list, and if you don't see more paying users you don't owe a penny.
Yup, no high consulting fees, contracts or blood oaths.
If I don't bring in revenue, you don't owe a thing.
I'm not sure if this is a problem you have, and if it's not, that's totally cool.
If it is, would you be open to a quick 7-minute chat?
I promise to be somewhat useful, and I might even tell you my favorite joke.   [Make your 'ask' - sugar coat it!]
Finally, it's time to release my favorite weapon.
A picture of my dog wearing a witches hat and a green tutu. Just what you've always wanted:   [Fun ending :)]
I await your stern but fair reply,                        [Anticipate negative replies]
Aaron Krall
Free Trial Conversion Specialist / SaaS Lover / Below Average Runner   [Fun with job title]
```
- **Why:** Buchan's notes mark seven moves: unusual greeting, amusing honest opener, "I help [X] without [X]", sugar-coated ask, fun ending, anticipate negative replies, fun with job title [PDF L04 p14]. **Steal:** the exaggerate-then-confess data line, and performance terms ("you don't owe a penny") inside "I help [X] without [X]", between two jokes. (He calls this "risk reversal": "this was key" [NL 2026-02-17].)
### 3. 8-Part Sequence, Email #1 (template) · [PDF 8-Part p1] · verbatim
```text
Greetings {{first name}},
You've never heard of me. Hi, I'm {firstname}. I got your email from {honest/funny reason here}.
Let me get right down to it…
I don't know if this is a problem you have, but I help {solution to problem}
I {what you do}
If you're even the slightest bit curious, would you be open to a quick 7-minute chat?
I promise to give you some ideas you're free to steal. If you play your cards right, I might even tell you the lamest joke I know!
Finally, it's time to release my favorite weapon.
A picture of my {pet name} {doing something cute} Just what you've always wanted:
I await your profanity-filled response.
Have an absolutely terrific day,
First Name Surname
{job title one} / {job title two} / {job title three - funniest}
(Remember to insert picture)
```
- **Why:** the drunk email compressed into slots. "7-minute" makes the ask small; "ideas you're free to steal" makes it a gain: "Make it look like they stand to gain, rather than being hard-sold too" [PDF LI-Templates p3]. **Steal:** an odd, specific number and a "lamest joke" bribe in place of a hard CTA.

### 5. Annette the accountant: the pie-chart "poll" · [PDF L04 p18] [L04 16:42] · verbatim (written for an accountant) · OCR-corrected (slide shows the opening only)
```text
Greetings Jacob,
You've never heard of me. (Hi, I'm Annette). It's a pleasure to make your inbox.
I'm better with spreadsheets than words. As such, let me get my point across using a beautiful pie-chart.
This isn't a real poll, but I imagine it's not far off...

● If I'm in trouble with the tax authorities
● When my business partner insists
● Once a quarter

```

## Follow-ups and replies

### 6. Email #2: "Sent from my iPhone" · [PDF 8-Part p1] · verbatim (template)
```text
Sorry, forgot to mention - you can find more information at our website (www.url.com) - I'll get back to nervously anticipating your response.
First Name
    -    Sent from my iPhone
(No Unsubscribe Link)
```
- **Why:** reads as a real, hurried afterthought (our analysis). **Flag the opt-out:** SKILL.md overrides "(No Unsubscribe Link)" where anti-spam law requires one. **Steal:** a one-line "forgot to mention" with one link and one self-mocking clause.
### 7. Email #3: the sad-face photo · [PDF 8-Part p1–2] · verbatim (template)
```text
Hey {{first}},
I'll be honest, I emailed you last week, and didn't get a reply and I'm… well, I'll let my photo do the talking.
This is me:
[Insert photo of you with a sad face]
If you could hit 'reply', even if it's to tell me to politely 'go away', it would put a smile on my face.
Fingers crossed!
Have a great day,
First name Surname
{job title 1} / {job title 2} / {job title 3}
```
- **Why:** a "go away" still counts as a win, so replying feels safe (our analysis); self-deprecation "is always going to help you" [L03 4:18]. **Steal:** an honest one-liner, a photo of yourself, and a CTA where "no" is welcome.
### 8. Email #6: three convenient calls to action · [PDF 8-Part p3]; slide version [PDF L03 p22] · verbatim (template)
```text
{{first_name}},
I know you're busy, so I've prepared 3 convenient calls to action for you:
    1. Ignore this email and eventually I'll get the picture and write terrible poetry about the deal we never did. {MOST POPULAR}
    2. Hit 'reply' and I'll do the same. {RECOMMENDED}
    3. WILDCARD - Call me on {x}. Interrupt my day like I have yours. I deserve it! {LIMITED TIME OFFER}
Option 2 is my favourite!
Have a great evening,
First Name
```
- **Why:** "There's something really honest" about labelling ignoring "most popular" [L03 23:52]; the funniest option goes first, against the usual rule [L03 22:51]. Slide variant: "Hit 'reply' instead of 'delete' and make my day, month and year. (RECOMMENDED)". **Steal:** make "ignore me" an official option, so the email admits it might fail.

## LinkedIn

### 9. The "not the generic invite" invite · [PDF LI-Templates p2] [L05 7:23] · verbatim
```text
Hey hey {firstname},
I really wanted to connect. As such, I've decided not to send you the generic LinkedIn invite!
Fingers crossed,
Jon
```
- **Why:** "Sending something a little more interesting and human works well at scale… This is the next best thing" to a personalised invite [PDF LI-Templates p2]. **Steal:** name the lazy default and say you skipped it.
### 10. The One/Two Punch: invite, then the accept message · [PDF LI-Templates p4–5] [L05 10:29] [L05 11:29] · verbatim
```text
[Invite]
Hey hey {firstname},
Nice to 'meet' you. I have a weird skill that means I can book meetings with just about anybody.
If you're at all curious, click 'accept'. :)
If not, send me your most cutting insult. :(
Fingers crossed,
Jon
[When they accept]
Hello there {firstname},
Thanks for not sending me a cutting insult!
If you're at all curious about the ideas I mentioned, let me know and I'll spill the beans.
I say 'ideas'… It is of course, a thinly veiled sales pitch. However, I often get too enthusiastic and give away great ideas. You should take advantage of this!
Have an absolutely wonderful day,
Jon
```
- **Why:** the insult line "adds humour to the call to action, gives them a way out, and sets up the follow up message"; message 2 "Immediately references the humour in the invite copy" ("Disarming honesty") [PDF LI-Templates p4–5]. "Nobody really does" send the insult [L05 11:29]. **Steal:** a two-step joke where message 2 pays off message 1. Replace "weird skill" with the sender's real offering.

   -     My name is {name} and I'm a {what you do}

You won't have heard of me. (Hi, I'm Jon!). I got your details from a list * gasp *. But hey, that means you're list-worthy; that's gotta be something, right? 😉

### 13. The press-release letter to Sony Music · [PDF L04 p16] [L04 13:39] · verbatim (address block omitted) · excerpt
```text
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE:
Charm Offensive CEO Reaches Out To Director of Communications of Sony Music (UK) Ltd
London, England — January 16, 2017 - In an unexpected move, Jon Buchan, CEO, Charm Offensive plc is believed to have sent a completely unsolicited letter to Indy Vee, Director of Communications, Sony Music (UK) Ltd. It is understood that Mr Buchan is offering advice, support and a "priceless" gift - a sticker containing an amusing picture of a dog wearing a top hat and a monocle.
…
"I'm not sure exactly what is going on at this point," said Ms Vee. "Until today, I'd never even heard of Mr Buchan or his brilliant agency."
…
Ms Vee has not yet decided whether to take the baffling but kindhearted approach seriously.
"I'm a little suspicious this whole affair is just a stunt to drum up business for Mr Buchan's agency."
At the time of reading, it is unknown whether Ms Vee will respond to Mr Buchan or just throw this piece of paper in the trash before getting on with actual work.
About
Charm Offensive is a daring agency filled with creative individuals far more talented than Mr Buchan. Our capacity to build spectacular, interactive, animated and sentient* content could be a useful weapon to add to your arsenal.
*We have not as yet been able to develop content capable of thoughts and feelings.
```
- **Why:** "part press release and part sort of Onion article"; it only works if they know the form: "if you send this format to someone that has never seen a press release, it's not going to work so well" [L04 14:40]. ### 16. The handwritten letter and the phone follow-up · [SITE 010] · verbatim (method + script)
```text
If you've had no response to your letter, follow up with an email one week later, with the subject line "handwritten letter."
Or call them on the phone. When your prospect asks who it is, you can say "Did you get a slightly weird handwritten letter last week?"

```

## Pitches to hosts and journalists

### 17. The podcast pitch · [PDF Podcasts-Template p1] [L06 4:08] · verbatim (template)
```text
{name},
I wanted to introduce myself in a way that shows I'm succinct, clever and witty. Alas, I wrote this email instead.
Put your story here…
[Identity · Struggle · Discovery · Surprise]
So why the hell am I writing to you?
1. I really like your podcast.
2. I think I would be a good guest. {put your reasoning here - why would you be a good guest?}

Fingers crossed. Either way, thanks for the podcast. Keep up the great work.

{yourname}
```
- **Subject he used:** "The drunk cold email that changed my life" [L06 4:08] (a podcast-pitch subject; unverified as a cold-email subject). **Why:** "Ego rub - but completely honest. ### 18. The journalist "premarketing" pitch · [PDF Premarketing p1–2] · verbatim (template)
```text
{firstname},
Just what you wanted - another pitch! Sort of…
I think I might have something that interests you - but you be the judge of that!
I'm from {companyname}. We {saywhatyoudohere}.
My idea is {idea for story/content idea here}.
I thought this might interest you because {reason here}.
I need your help…
Please pick from one of the following convenient responses. Is this idea:
1. Awesome. Do it! I'd write about/publish this in a heartbeat!
2. It's not bad... but needs work. (Please feel free to embellish here. Be as polite or scathing as you see fit. I have a think skin!) [sic]
3. AWFUL! Go back to the drawing board, you chump!
Thanks for reading this far.
Even if you just reply with just 1, 2 or 3, I'd be most grateful.
Have a great day,
{yourname}
```
- **Why:** asking for a verdict, not a favour, means journalists "are involved in the process from the start" [PDF Premarketing p1]; option 3 lets them be rude, so replying costs nothing (our analysis). **Steal:** "Even if you just reply with just 1, 2 or 3": a one-keystroke reply.

## Drafting checklist
1. **Pick the closest example by channel and stage** (cold / nudge / reply / LinkedIn / letter / host pitch), then borrow its *moves*, not its words.
2. **Keep the order of beats:** Way In → hinge ("Let me get right down to it…") → one-line offer → sugar-coated ask with an out → fun ending → closer → three titles.
3. **Swap every specific** (names, pets, clients, numbers, "weird skill") for the sender's real ones. Never borrow a result, client or stat; keep an opt-out where the law requires it (#6).
4. **Attribute correctly:** Aaron's email is a student example; Jess and Annette are Buchan writing for others; the ferret-letter text is a third-party transcription.
5. **Match the stage's tone:** follow-ups stay charming and give value; replies to warm prospects drop the irreverence (T2 in follow-ups-and-replies.md).
6. **Formats only for audiences that know them** (press release, bingo card, pie chart); a letter's follow-up email names the oddity and covers "if you didn't get the letter".
