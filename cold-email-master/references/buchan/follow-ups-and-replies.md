# Follow-ups & Replies (runtime reference)

What happens after the first cold touch: the nudges that chase a reply, then every kind of reply through to a booked call and a proposal. Templates are verbatim from the t10 deck ("Conversations to Cash: I got a positive reply, now what?!"). The full 8-part copy is in cold-email.md; this file only maps each nudge to the reply it fishes for. Anything under **Not covered by Buchan — applied** is our inference, never his copy.

## Model
- **The chain exists to get a call,** then a proposal. Little yeses build: reply → call → they send info → you send ideas → proposal. "Use what makes sense - remove what doesn't" [PDF t10 p2].
- **Five reactions to a charming pitch** [SITE 031]: (1) ignore everything, "You can't win them all"; (2) amused, but "now isn't the right time"; (3) amused, start to reply, get distracted; (4) delighted, ask for a call or the free ideas; (5) angry and "VERY IMPORTANT" (the smallest group).
- **Follow-ups exist for type 3.** Busy prospects "can be forgiven for neglecting to respond … The onus is on you to remind them you exist" [SITE 115].

## Cadence (detail in cold-email.md §8-part sequence)
- **8 emails, then stop; ≥5 working days apart, in-thread.** Course: one a week, "And then I'll get the picture" [L03 33:14], as "common courtesy" [SITE 115]; others' 3–4 days he once tolerated ("You do you"). By 2026: "I recommend leaving 5 working days between each follow-up message. Some people send them at a faster rate than this but I believe this is too fast." "You're a guest in their inbox"; sending manually, reply to your last email "keeping the copy from your previous emails intact" [NL 2026-02-02]. Follow-ups are short: "nothing but a 'bump'" [NL 2023-01-13].
- **Every nudge gives value and/or entertains,** "punchy, interesting, funny". Never: "You didn't reply to my email I sent 2 days ago. Are you interested?" Ask it "in a way that doesn't infuriate" [SITE 115].
- **A few negative replies to follow-ups are "fair game. You are an uninvited guest in their inbox"** [SITE 115].
- **After the sequence ends,** "you can send them another email in a month or two" [INT fearless-business 28:14].
- **(unverified — legacy):** "Email #2 a few hours/1 day later" and "steps a few days apart".

## Follow-up type map

| # / type | Shape | Reply it fishes for |
|---|---|---|
| #2 "Forgot to mention" | One line + website link, "nervously anticipating your response", first name, "Sent from my iPhone", "(No Unsubscribe Link)" (**flag the opt-out**, SKILL.md) | Reads like a personal note, so they answer like one |
| #3 Sad-face photo | "I'll be honest, I emailed you last week, and didn't get a reply" + sad photo + "even if it's to tell me to politely 'go away'" | **Any** reply, a "go away" included |
| #4 The sensible one | "trying a more sensible and professional approach" + topical stat + link + why it matters to them | Value; "accidentally hit 'reply' instead of 'delete'" |
| #5 Head-desk picture | "I really hope I'm not causing you to do this…" + image; reply "if you'd like to talk … or not" | A yes or a no, both welcome |
| #6 Three convenient CTAs | Ignore {MOST POPULAR} / reply {RECOMMENDED} / call me {LIMITED TIME OFFER} | Reply as the "recommended" option |
| #7 Pure smile | Amusing picture + "Hope this brightens up your day". No ask | Goodwill; a reply on their own initiative |
| #8 Breakup | "may have jumped the gun", "this will be my last follow up", "if I'm mistaken … send me a quick email" | Last chance for the distracted type 3 |
| Letter follow-up | Subject names the letter's oddity; "If you didn't get the letter … Do let me know" | 80%+ opens; "didn't get it" doubles as a reply prompt |
| Handwritten-letter follow-up | "one week later, with the subject line 'handwritten letter.'" Or phone: "Did you get a slightly weird handwritten letter last week?" | Recognition |

- **The breakup.** The course's #8 is the **plainest** email: courtesy and a clear end [PDF 8-Part p4]. Architecture B calls for a "Funny breakup email" [PDF L04 p29], but no funny breakup copy exists in the sources. After the breakup, stop.
- **Sold but not shown** (use as briefs, never quote as his copy): sequences that poke fun at following up, "quench any possibility of an angry response", add a new absurd "bonus" each time, compare your price with ridiculous products [SITE 115]; Grief Emails 4–6 (Depression, Acceptance, Break-Up) are blurbs only [NL 2025-06-07 18-effective-charming-templates]. Don't write them as his.

**5 Stages of Grief, Emails 1–3 (verbatim)** [NL 2026-02-02]. Shape: no-reply admission → melodrama aimed at *yourself* → `*ahem* [X] aside, I'd love to…` → one-line ask → opt-out. Fishes for any reply. Line breaks condensed; words untouched; the source is clipped mid-Email 3, so never complete it.
```text
Email 1: Denial — Subject: oh my... no reply :(
{firstname}, I haven’t had a reply from you as yet. As you might imagine, this has made me sad.
(Optional: Selfie of you looking slightly sad.) Grief-stricken, even... I’ve been thinking:
“How can it be?! How could {firstname} resist the urge to respond to my completely unsolicited sales message? Ahhh Ha! S/he must not have received it. That must be it. I’ll send a friendly follow up...”
(Ah yes, the first stage of grief is denial.)
*ahem* Absurd lamentations aside, I’d love to talk to you about {X}. Would you be up for a quick chat? :-)
If you’re not at all interested, my apologies. please click here* so you don’t receive any further attempts at funny follow up emails from me.
Have an absolutely wonderful day, {yourname}

Email 2: Anger — Subject: I messed up...
Hello {firstname}. Me again.
I’ve not had a reply from you, so I’ve been busying myself going through the 5 stages of grief. Today... I am at ANGER.
(Optional: Photo of you looking faux-angry) Here goes...
“Why did I think it was a clever idea to send a weird email {firstname} out of the blue, and get a response? Damn it. You’ve ruined it, {yourname}!”
*ahem* Comical overreactions aside, I’d love to chat to you about {X}. Up for a quick call? :-)
Have an absolutely wonderful day, {yourname}
P.S. If you’d like to never hear from me again, click this link as hard as you can. FYI: This link, for reasons unbeknownst to me, also makes a large novelty-sized hand slap me around the face with impressive force.

Email 3: Bargaining — Subject: ok... you got me, {firstname}.
{firstname}, Alas, no reply as yet. As such, I am forced to go through the 3rd stage of grief: Bargaining.
(Optional: Selfie of you mock begging.) Here goes...
“Please. I’ll do anything. I’ll do you a free {X}, on the house... I promise It’ll be the most majestic {X} you’ve read/watched all month. How does that sound?”
*ahem* Desperate attempts at bargaining aside, I’d love to have a chat about {X}.
If not of interest, my apologies. Feel free to click here* to [source clipped here]
```

## Reply triage (classify first, then answer what they actually asked — "use good judgment")

| Reaction | Response | Template |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive, wants a call** ("When are you free?") | Thank them; ask when works for *them*, booking link as the "Alternatively"; "looking forward". If they named a slot, answer that | T1 |
| **Positive, wants the free ideas** ("What info do you need?") | Thank them, then move it to a call | T1, then Booking |
| **"Tell me more"** (interested, no call yet) | Short bulleted intro, one success-story link, a **10-minute** ask + booking link | T2 |
| **"Not right now", with a date** ("try me in September") | Accept it; set a CRM **and** diary reminder; CRM stage "slow burn". Don't ask 3/6/9 | T3b (applied) |
| **"Not right now", no date** (busy, under contract) | Thank them; ask the 3/6/9-month question with a promised cute extra; optional newsletter ask; LinkedIn invite **after** they answer | T3, T4 |
| **Amused but "I don't need a [X]"** | Thank them for the kind words; ask to email again in 3 or 6 months | T3 |
| **Negative, polite** | Make sure they're unsubscribed; send the one-line apology | T5 |
| **Negative, rude / "be more professional!"** | Unsubscribe; **don't reply**. They disqualified themselves: "BONUS!" | none |
| **Late reply, months later** | Charming emails get remembered ("A whole year later") [SITE 033]. Treat as positive | T1 |
| Send more info · forwarded to a colleague · "what will it cost?" · out-of-office · no-show | Not covered by Buchan | see Applied |

**Signals before the reply:** "This made my day" and a colleague-forward ("LOL we need this person") are buying signals; treat them as positive [NL 2025-07-10 when-a-prospect-says-this]. **Diagnose the mix, not the message.** Only if replies are *all* negative, "look at your data, copy and offer" [PDF t10 p3]. Otherwise, "It's just words on the internet."

## Reply templates (verbatim; swap "Jon" and the Calendly link for the sender's)

**T1 — Positive → book the call** [PDF t10 p5]. The order matters: their time first, the link second. No call length is set here (legacy "7–15 min" is a blend; don't use it).
```text
Hey {name},

Thank you for replying!

I'd love to chat sometime! Could you let me know when works for you?

Alternatively, book in some time at https://calendly.com/charmoffensive

I am looking forward to talking to you! :)

Thanks so much,

Jon
```
The slide continues: "Obviously, if they've made further comments or have asked specific questions," (cut off). The lesson finishes it: answer those, "use good judgment" [L10 5:14].

**T2 — "Tell me more"** [PDF t10 p6]. The absurd third service is confessed in a footnote; "solve X without Y" is the Session 3 formula; drop the success-story line if there isn't one. "10 minute" is deliberately small: "You want it to not seem like a big commitment" [L10 6:18].
```text
Hey {name},

Thank you for replying! Here's a brief introduction to [companyname]. I'll be brief and use bullet points:

  ●     We are a daring digital marketing agency offering copywriting, social media advertising and bedroom tidying*.
  ●     We are based in [X] and have [X] wonderful staff.
  ●     We can help you [solve problem] without [pain point you avoid]. You can learn how we [result here] for [client name]
        by clicking here.

I would love a 10 minute chat on the phone.

Could you let me know when works for you? Alternatively, book in some time at https://calendly.com/charmoffensive

Thank you so much for your time.

Cheers,

Jon Buchan
Director / Tea Maker / Interim Head of Stationery :)
*We don't actually offer bedroom tidying. Apologies for getting your hopes up!
```

**T3 — "Not right now" (only if they gave no date)** [PDF t10 p4]. Pick the interval that fits the sales cycle; he used to offer "three or six months" and let them choose. Then set a reminder in CRM **and** diary ("a double whammy").
```text
May I contact you in 3/6/9 months to see if anything has changed? I promise to include [something cute/amusing/interesting here].
```
Optional, if there's a mailing list:
```text
Mind if I add you to our newsletter? It contains [X] and I think you'd find it useful because [X]. If not, no worries of course! Don't ask - don't get! :)
```

**T3b: "Not right now", with a date.** *Applied (our inference, consistent with [PDF t10 p4] [L10 2:03] and the tone shift [SITE 031]); Buchan gives no template.*
```
Hi {first name},

Totally understand. {Their busy reason, in their words}, so that should get your full attention.

I'll pop back in {their date, in their own words, e.g. "in Q1"} with those ideas, sharpened.
{Optional, one light line: "I'll even have a fresh joke by then. No promises on quality."}

Have a great {week/run-up to Q1},
{Sender}
```
Rules: accept with no pushback, mirror *their* date in their own words ("in Q1"), and never convert it to a guessed date,
keep at most one light line, then tell the user to set the CRM + diary reminder.

**T4 — LinkedIn invite after an email reply.** Not-right-now version [PDF t10 p4], sent only **after** they answer the 3/6/9 question ("Don't do it before you've got a response"):
```text
Hey {name}, thanks for replying to my email. I'd love to connect with you on LinkedIn, if you don't mind. Thank you, Jon.
```
Booked-call version [PDF t10 p9], once they've accepted a call and you aren't connected:
```text
Thanks for replying to my email, {name}. I hope you don't mind, but would love to connect on here!

Thank you,

Jon
```

**T5 — Negative, polite** [PDF t10 p3]. Only if they were polite. If they're "super mean", don't reply: "You're just going to risk making them even more angry even if you're apologetic" [L10 1:01]. Always unsubscribe them.
```text
Apologies, {name}. I won't contact you again. Best of luck with everything. Have a great week, Jon.
```

## Tone shift after a positive reply
- **The rule.** "Don't think you have to continue the irreverent tone of your opening gambit any further. I never did. Good lord – can you imagine how annoying that would be? Instead, show your enthusiasm – and demonstrate your knowledge." [SITE 031] (Rubric H8.)
- **Why.** "It takes gumption and charm to open opportunity, but it takes more to get people to open their wallets. Knowledge. Credibility. Enthusiasm." [SITE 024]
- **Newsletter version.** "Humour gets you through the door. Enthusiasm and knowledge get you the deal." [NL 2022-12-12 lets-quash-some-objections]. On the call "you don't have to be funny. Nobody will expect this" [NL 2023-06-24 unusual-offer]. "Enthusiasm is the single most cost-effective cosmetic there is." [NL 2026-02-17].
- **Not humourless.** His reply templates keep **one** light beat: the bedroom-tidying footnote and silly title (T2), the promised cute extra (T3), the "toddler's handwriting" line when asking to record. The irreverence goes; warmth plus one small smile stays. Candour carries over.
- **Keep the informality they hand back** *(our reading: warmth and light banter, not escalating gags)*. "Many of them will try and outdo your humor ... Just continue that momentum" [INT fearless-business 13:42]. They know it's "an opening gambit" [INT fearless-business 12:39]. If you pitched, "blow them away" on substance: humour gets you shortlisted [INT yt-drunk-cold-email-changed-life 19:13].

## Booking mechanics → discovery call → proposal
- **Scheduling link:** Calendly; separate links per day/length. "Maximise your availability" when starting out, "so you make it as easy as possible for people to talk to you" [L10 7:20].
- **Medium:** Zoom (it records), Skype, or "the good ol' telephone" [PDF t10 p8]. Nervous? "Perhaps, start with a call rather than an in-person meeting" [SITE 016].
- **CRM stages** (he used Salesflare): **Contacted** (only once they've "agreed really to do a call") → **Slow burn** (3/6/9-month people) → **Qualified** (they want a proposal) → **Proposal made** → **Won / Lost**. No "Lead" stage for cold email.
- **Before the call:** their website, socials, Wikipedia, news, competitors, LinkedIn profile (background, recommendations, shared interests). The site "won't be a perfect representation": learn "enough not to make any silly mistakes" ("I just love your excellent skin care products!" / "We don't have any…"). For big clients, offer an NDA up front. **Don't assume budget from glamour:** his luxury-brand versus dull-manufacturer guesses "were all assumptions" [INT fearless-business 30:24]; ask, don't presume.
- **On the call** (adapt every question to the offer) [PDF t10 p14–27]:
  - "Keep them talking!" Record? "Do you mind if I record this conversation? My handwriting is like that of a toddlers! If not, no worries of course!" [sic]
  - Intro: "I'd love to hear directly from you about what you do and how you got started? Mind giving me the elevator pitch?"
  - Goals: "What's your 'picture of success' for this year?" History: what they do now, how it worked, past suppliers (ex-supplier pain becomes an objection to calm in the proposal).
  - Pitch yourself briefly ("[X] without the [pain]", named clients, "that is where you come in!"), then "Just thinking out loud, but have you considered …?"
  - Subtle proposal: a free "mini audit" or "brainstorm some brief ideas … and send them over, if you'd like?" Newsletter bait: a *blurred* mini-audit screenshot, built only once they say "Yeah, send it over" [NL 2024-09-19 cant-i-just-edit]; "They are expecting you to trash them. You shouldn't." [NL 2023-06-01]
  - "Would you mind if I put a short deck/document together for you?" + "I understand if this information is too sensitive" → move to **Qualified**.
  - KPIs; "This may sound a bit cheeky, but I have to ask… 'How do I impress you?'"; budget "Even if it's ballpark…" (won't say → tiered proposal); decision makers ("alas, it is my job…"), only if they've been forthcoming.
  - Close: "I can get you a proposal done for Friday. How does that sound?" → "Excellent. Would you at all mind if we set up a call to run through it next week? I promise it will only take 15 minutes of your time." Local or big: "A call is fine, but if you can, I'd love to come and meet you in person. If that's not possible, not to worry."
- **After the call** [PDF t10 p29]: email their projects/goals/challenges ("set the scene", a sentence or a few, "Don't write war and peace"), a brief outline of how you help + case-study links, what the proposal will contain, when to expect it, and the booked call ("I'm looking forward to catching up on {day date time}"). The promised "post call email" template is **not in the sources**.
- **After a pitch meeting:** "Follow up with a thank you email as soon as possible … Ask when you think it would be OK to follow up with them. It shows you care, you are prompt, and you are reliable." [PDF t11 p28–29]

## Objections (only what the sources say)

| Objection / friction | What Buchan does |
|---|---|
| "A call will be a hard sell / waste of time" | Pre-empt in the pitch: "ideas you're free to steal", so "there's going to be less objections"; sugar-coat the ask |
| Bad past supplier | Ask about it on the call, then "calm that objection in your proposal" |
| Industry pain (contracts, fees) | State the challenges you avoid: "We won't tie you into a long term contract", part performance-based, no huge set-up fees [PDF t10 p21] |
| Won't reveal budget | Tiered proposal: "If I don't have an idea of budget, I give three tiers", but "Don't give too many options" |
| "Can we trust your claims?" | Offer past clients as references (ask them first). "Not many people actually do contact them but it shows confidence" |
| Too sensitive to share data | "I understand if this information is too sensitive at this stage" |
| "You're too small / inexperienced" (your fear) | "If you're nervous in a meeting, tell them. Be honest … It shows you care." Promise "what I thought was likely" [SITE 016] |
| "You should be more professional!" | Not an objection to answer: they "disqualify themselves" |
| "How do I know you'll work hard?" | State aligned self-interest: "I want results just as much as you ... because I'm going to use this case study" [INT eofire 31:19] [INT eofire 32:20] |
| *Too small; humour is unprofessional* | Small = "faster and more flexible" [NL 2023-04-06 negatives]; "Nobody becomes the CEO of say, RedBull, and then decides, "You know what, I don't like to laugh anymore. …"" [NL 2022-12-12 lets-quash-some-objections] |
| Price negotiation | Name it with candour: "this is deliberately inflated because I know you're going to negotiate me down" [INT dropdeadcopy-ep180 11:31]; cf. his "Of course it is! I know you're going to negotiate down!" [PDF L09 p37] |

**Sold but not in the sources, so don't fabricate:** Closing With Charm's answers on price, timeframes, experience, results and methodology; the "absurd scenario" objection technique; "What will it cost?"; proposal chasing; rescheduling cancelled calls.

## Negative replies
- **Expect them;** you're "still interrupting someone's day". One negative ≠ stop: it's "unforgivable" to stop a campaign with "an inbox full of positive responses" after one. "Don't get upset at words on the internet" [SITE 012].
- **They're useful.** They disqualify "self-important, stern, angry-jobsworth prospects" unlikely to "pay your invoices on time" [SITE 033]: "you're never going to have to speak to them ever again. BONUS!" [SITE 031]
- **Always remove anyone who says no** [PDF t10 p3], even though #2 is sent "(No Unsubscribe Link)".
- His list-unsubscribe page ("Cajolement ceased! … YOU WIN THIS ROUND! 😀") is list marketing, not a cold reply; for cold prospects use T5.

## Not covered by Buchan — applied
Label every draft built from this section as our inference (CLAUDE.md). None of it is his copy.
- **Stop the sequence on any reply.** The nudges exist to get a reply; once one arrives, triage takes over.
- **Funny breakup (architecture B).** Same three beats as #8, delivered with a gag: own the presumption, announce the end, leave one easy way back. Never present it as "Buchan's breakup".
- **"Send me more info."** Treat as "Tell me more" (T2): 3 bullets, one proof link, the 10-minute ask. No brochure or feature dump; the job is to sell the call, "Not to give every little detail" [SITE 034].
- **Forwarded / "talk to X".** A forward is "tacit endorsement" [SITE 033]; his favourite reply was "My colleague forwarded me your spam email and we would like to meet you" [SITE 001]. Thank the forwarder; write to the new contact as a warm intro naming who passed it on (don't re-send the cold email); ask for a short call (T1/T2); connect with both on LinkedIn.
- **"What will it cost?"** Say honestly it depends on what they need; give a range only if the user has one; steer to the 10-minute call, where budget is asked properly and a tiered proposal can follow. (Guest view, not Buchan: "quick and dirty" pricing requests often "flake out" [SITE 036].)
- **Out-of-office.** Not a reply. Note the return date; let the sequence run, or pause until they're back.
- **No-show / cancelled call.** Short, blame-free, lightly self-deprecating (sad-face spirit); two new times plus the booking link. Never "you missed our call".
- **LinkedIn nudges and DM replies.** Adapt the email nudges: no subject, short, ≥5 working days apart (his 2026 email cadence). Use the triage and T1–T5 with no subject line or email signature, keep the booking link, cut T2 to 2–3 lines, and skip T4 (already connected).
- **Post-call email.** Build from the after-the-call outline above; keep it short.
- **Answering a pasted objection.** (1) acknowledge it honestly; (2) one relevant proof point or "challenge we avoid"; (3) re-offer the small next step (call, mini audit, ideas) with an easy out. Warm, not irreverent.
- **Polite opt-out on a warm list.** One light touch in the "Cajolement ceased!" spirit is fine; for cold prospects, T5 only.

## Drafting checklist
1. **Classify** the reply against the triage table and say which row. Mixed replies ("not now, but tell me more") get both parts answered.
2. **Answer their actual words first,** then use the template.
3. **Positive reply:** irreverence dropped; enthusiasm and knowledge shown; one small smile at most (rubric H8).
4. **Ask is small, with an out:** their time first, the link as the alternative; 10 minutes for "tell me more".
5. **Not right now:** 3/6/9 ask only if no date given; end with a dated CRM + diary reminder for the user; LinkedIn invite only after they answer.
6. **Negative:** rude gets no draft; polite gets T5 verbatim (sender's name swapped) plus a reminder to unsubscribe them.
7. **Follow-up nudge:** one job per email, a fresh benefit or gag, ≥5 working days apart and in-thread, no nagging; #2's missing opt-out flagged (prefer a Buchan-voiced opt-out, banks.md).
8. **Applied drafts** (send-more-info, referral, pricing, OOO, no-show, funny breakup, LinkedIn DM reply, post-call email) are labelled as our inference.
9. **Drafts only.** Never send or schedule anything.
