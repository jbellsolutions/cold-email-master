# Story, Ideal Clients & Offer (runtime reference)

Use this to build or fill the sender profile and to find the angle. "You" can mean the person or their company. Lines marked **Applied** are our synthesis, not Buchan's.

## 1. The goal: "the perfect match"

- **Show three things:** you're good at what you do and passionate about it; you can solve their problem or reach their goal; you'll work well together. Bonus: you know their sector, its jargon and its trends.
- **The offer is one of three variables you control** (with data and copy). Ask whether you've given them "a good reason to think a call or meeting with you isn't an awful use of their time?" [PDF L09 p34].

## 2. The five story questions (Lesson 1 worksheet)

| Question | Prompt | Buchan's own answers (models) | Feeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Great at? | Up to 5 core skills | Cold pitching / Funny copy / Digital marketing / Creative thinking / Interacting with people | Credibility line |
| Stand for? | 3 values. Optional pattern: craft, helping people, a value | "Great creative work…", "Being effective…", "Honesty - I don't use unethical practices. There is no trickery." | Bio, posts |
| Against? | Up to 3, ideally problems in your sector | "'big' 'lazy' agencies - Coasting on retainers", "fancy offices ... but no substance", "Dishonesty ... Putting profit before people." | Pain-point emails, PR angles |
| Obstacles faced? | Optional pattern: industry problem, a struggle that resonates, an advantage you lacked | "Struggled for sales when I launched my own agency", "Didn't have the budget to compete" | The **Struggle** beat; story posts |
| Ambitions? | "Try not to make this about money." Pattern: self, others, overarching | "To make business communications human, honest and enjoyable" | Bullet 4 of the five-bullet list |

**Rules for the answers**
- **The patterns are optional.** Take a position without turning bitter.
- **Genuine or nothing.** "I'd rather you not have as much content ... than include something that you can't defend" [L01 7:20].
- **Turn a weakness into the angle.** Little experience? "Call out the elephant in the room" and frame it as enthusiasm, because "people like to help people that really want to get ahead" [L01 35:49]. A day job can be part of the story: be honest about it, and test it.

## 3. The story formula: Identity → Struggle → Discovery → Surprise (ISDS)

- **It isn't Buchan's invention.** Kevin Rogers named it; Buchan used it "by accident". Don't call it "Buchan's formula" [L06 8:26].
- **It's a guide.** "If it doesn't fit perfectly into that, don't worry too much."
- **Keep it short and end on the present.** Tell it in a few lines, then say what you do now and give a CTA. On LinkedIn, keep Identity light.

**His drunk-email story mapped two ways.** Both mappings are his; use whichever fits the material.

| Beat | Lesson 6 version | Reddit deck version |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | "I used to run a digital marketing agency" | "Desperate for sales" |
| Struggle | "about a year in, my word of mouth leads dried up" | "Hellishly drunk" |
| Discovery | "desperate and drunk, I wrote a completely absurd cold email ... and I sent it" | "Wrote cold email with inhibitions low" |
| Surprise | "amazingly it worked" | "It worked" |

The ferret picture is *not* the Surprise. It's the curiosity hook.

**What makes a story work** (his analysis)
- **Accident and novelty.** Accidental beats deliberate, "especially when it's life changing". An oxymoronic title ("drunk cold email" + "changed my life") is reusable.
- **Honesty is the pattern interrupt.** Example: "I was desperate for sales and got drunk".
- **Prove it isn't hype.** Quote a real reply, then add "This is completely true by the way!"
- **Specific visual detail** ("a little cocker spaniel dog"). Few words, chosen details.
- **Punch up, or punch the villain** from your "against" list. Ask whether the prospect can be part of the story ("The Ambitious Underdog"), and whether it breaks "us and them".
- **Talk about yourself, captivatingly.** "When everyone else said 'Don't talk about yourself', I wrote my story but in a captivating way" [PDF L09 p35]. This overrides the usual advice.

## 4. Pitch to a host or organiser (podcast/speaking)

Moves in order:
1. An oxymoronic subject.
2. An unusual typewriter font.
3. An honest ego rub ("I just discovered it recently"). Never pretend to be a long-time fan.
4. Proof attachments, named with light humour ("This probably shows my insecurity"), which makes people scroll.
5. The ISDS story in a few lines, plus a curiosity tease ("it involved this picture of a ferret and bunny ears").
6. Name-drops and repetition ("I'd accidentally been clever").
7. What you do now.
8. A bold "**So why the hell am I writing to you?**" and five bullets.
9. A silly close: "Ok. I hope that wasn't too painful. Thanks for reading. Fingers crossed" / "Stay vigilant" / "Chief of Staff".

Cut weak filler; he'd scrap "this is a bit of a ramble. Apologies". Prerequisite: "Unless you're a well-known name, you need to bring something to the table" (value, entertainment or news).

**The five-bullet list (verbatim).** It answers "What do I want? Why should they care?"

| # | Podcast version | Speaking-gig version | Job of the bullet |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "I really like your podcast." | "I've seen you have an event coming up on {date} called {event}. It looks interesting - the content looks great!" | Honest reason you chose them |
| 2 | "I think I would be a good guest. {put your reasoning here - why would you be a good guest?}" | "I'd love to be a guest speaker! (For free of course!) I can give a 10-20 minute talk on {topic here} that will complement the existing talks." | What you want, plus credibility |
| 3 | "I think I can offer {something useful and entertaining} to your audience" | Same | Why they should care |
| 4 | "I want to {put your mission here}" | Same | Your ambition (§2 Ambitions) |
| 5 | "I can't end this list on number 4. 5 bullets just looks and feels better." | Same | Self-aware joke, "a little bit of light humor" |

- **For speaking:** offer to speak free, on a complementary topic that isn't "done a thousand times before", and personalise it per event.
- **Applied:** in a cold sales email the same list can carry the offer. Bullet 2 is "I help [X] without [Y]", bullet 3 is their gain, bullet 4 is your ambition. Buchan only shows the list in podcast and speaking pitches.

## 5. Ideal clients

- **Who:** the decision-makers you can help most. Prioritise sectors you're excited by. **Segment; don't chase any budget.** You'll "have more in common" with them [L01 9:26]. Location can be global.
- **Profile variables:** job titles, locations, sectors, company size and keywords (traits, skills, niche terms). His keywords: "Disruptive, Ambitious, Humor, Humour, Funny, Enthusiastic, Maverick, Rule-breaker, Creative thinker".
- **Echo self-identified traits.** People who list "ambitious" self-identify with it, so reuse the word in the message (Liking).
- **Timing trigger:** "Changed jobs in past 90 days". New starters "haven't got the inertia" [L01 15:37]. Not on LinkedIn? Try Facebook groups, trade press and newsletters.

## 6. The offer

- **Pains you solve.** Cover the general ones ("Lack of new sales calls") and the problems with existing solutions in your market ("Agencies lock you into long term retainers").
- **Pains you help them avoid.** "No long term contracts", "No sleazy selling". This is the sibling of your "against" list.
- **Tie benefits to time, money or less stress** (optional). Example: "More sales calls / meetings -> More closed deals -> More cash".
- **The core line: "I help [X] without [Y]".** The slide reads "I help [X] without [X]"; the second slot is a pain point. Example (Aaron Krall's email, from the slide):
  > I help software companies convert their list of non-paying, expired trial users into paid users with ongoing email nurturing ... if you don't see more paying users you don't owe a penny. Yup, no high consulting fees, contracts or blood oaths.
  - The avoided pain can carry a triple joke.
  - Performance terms are optional: "not everyone can do a performance-based offer".
  - Buchan's own one-liner: "I can help people book more client meetings, open more sales opportunities, but without the flashy offices [price] tag or the massive retainers."
- **Fears and guarantees.** "Risk reversal" is his term (newsletters): "Include some 'risk reversal' in your message. Offer a free audit, brainstorming session, or other quick value you can deliver in 20 minutes. As charming as my original drunk cold email was, this was key." [NL 2026-02-17]; the unusual offer "reverses the risk for the prospect" [NL 2023-06-24 unusual-offer]. "No-brainer hook" is still **(unverified — legacy)**. The course adds: "Acknowledge their fears, and then quash them mercilessly" [SITE 024]. Build mechanisms against those fears "as long as you don't expose yourself to too much risk. Don't give them too many freebies." Keep the process simple enough to parrot to a boss.
- **Proof.** Real reply screenshots. Big names across sectors plus small ones (pre-empt "I bet it won't work for XXXX"). Third-party posts beat self-report. Testimonials: draft one for the client to approve. **Never invent proof.**
- **People before pain points.** Charm "talks to people, not pain points" [SITE 008]. **Applied:** in a cold email, lead with the human hook and keep the offer to one line. Pain detail goes in follow-ups and proposals.

## 7. Where each element goes

| Asset | Element | How |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn headline | Client + offer | One line: who you help and how. "I help founders stand out, book more sales calls and build their influence online" |
| LinkedIn bio | Story + offer | ISDS, informal, "Refreshing honesty is welcome", a link, a CTA |
| LinkedIn invite / follow-up | Offer, sector, keywords | `I have a weird skill that means I can {outcome}` · "My passion is {sector}…" · their bio keywords (see linkedin.md) |
| LinkedIn posts | Obstacles, ambitions, against | Stories and speaking "the truth". "Don't pitch all the time!" Hide any pitch with humour |
| Magic cold email | Offer | "I help [X] without [Y]" + an easy out |
| Podcast / speaking / PR | Story, ambition, against | ISDS + five bullets; a BrewDog-style stand; premarketing |

**The 8-part map** [PDF L04 p28]: 1 Intro and how you help · 2 Benefit 1 · 3 Against / pains avoided · 4 Benefit 2 · 5 Success story · 6 Against / pains avoided · 7 Benefit 3 · 8 Breakup.
That is architecture **A** ("spread out the sales pitch"). The verbatim 8-part template in cold-email.md is mostly architecture B ("funny each time"). Either way, don't sell everything in email 1. **Key accounts:** hyper-personalise: why you love their brand, how long you've been a customer, and a hint at your ideas for them.

## 8. PR angles (when the user wants coverage, not a call)

- **Use your "against" for contentious, humorous takes** that poke fun at your industry's jargon. A boring topic done "differently and more honestly" can still be news. Avoid "an ad in disguise".
- **Premarket the idea with journalists before you build it.** Template (verbatim):
  - "I'm from {companyname}. We {saywhatyoudohere}. My idea is {idea}. I thought this might interest you because {reason here}."
  - Then an easy three-option reply: "1. Awesome. Do it! ... 2. It's not bad... but needs work ... 3. AWFUL! Go back to the drawing board, you chump!"
  - "Even if you just reply with just 1, 2 or 3, I'd be most grateful."

## 9. Persuasion (only as Buchan applies it)

- **Impact first.** "Without IMPACT … nobody is listening." **Liking:** shared attributes and genuine common ground. **Authority and consensus:** case studies and known clients.
- **Reciprocity.** It applies to recommendations and to value-first offers: a smile makes them feel "compelled to respond ... because of the persuasive power of reciprocation" [NL 2024-09-26 the-potent-persuasive-positive]; of the £0 invoice, "This is reciprocity in action." [NL 2025-07-09 polite-bribe]. In the course, "ideas you're free to steal" is framed as the prospect standing to gain.
- **Scarcity and commitment are proposal-only** (time window, trial period). Keep scarcity out of cold outreach (repo rule: CTAs stay low-pressure).

## Drafting checklist

1. Are all five story answers present, and is every one genuine and defensible? If not, ask; never invent.
2. Is the ISDS story told in a few lines, ending with what they do now and a CTA? Is Surprise = "it worked"?
3. Is there an honest, unusual line near the start, such as a struggle admitted plainly?
4. Is the offer one line of "I help [X] without [Y]", tied to time, money or stress, with no fake guarantee?
5. Is the proof real (replies, names across sectors, third-party), with nothing invented?
6. Does it reuse the prospect's sector or self-identified keywords (Liking)?
7. Does the ask sell only the idea that a call "is not a terrible idea", leaving the detail for later touches?
8. For a host or organiser: are there five bullets answering "What do I want? Why should they care?", with the fifth a joke?
9. In a sequence: does each email draw on a different benefit, "against" item or story element?
