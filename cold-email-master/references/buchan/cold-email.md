# Cold Email & Direct Mail (runtime reference)

Templates are verbatim from the course PDFs. Joke mechanics are in voice-and-humour.md; offer and story are in story-and-offer.md.
**Recency rule:** the course is ~2017, the newsletters 2021–2026. Where they conflict, follow his latest stated practice and keep the course figure as history (knowledge/00 §0).

## Model
- **The email's only job is to win a call.** "The job of the cold email is to sell the idea that a phone call or a meeting with you is not a terrible idea", not to sell everything [L04 26:46]. This overrides advice to explain the full value proposition. Impact first, because most emails fail by going "straight to persuasion".
- **Spacing.** Funny, Serious, Funny, Serious: a laugh before each request. Read it aloud and cut joke-only stretches. "Be funny. If you can't be funny, be clever. If you can't be clever, keep that shit short." [NL 2025-06-08 freebie-alert-clever]. Aim for a smirk "within the first 10 seconds".
- **If it fails, check data, copy, offer and tech.** If every reply is negative, look at data, copy and offer.

## Data, targeting, volume
- **Setup and data.** Use a separate sending domain only when emailing hundreds (it guards against blacklisting). Use Gmail plus any of Mailshake, Quickmail, Reply.io or GMass to automate sends *and* follow-ups; by 2026 he names "a cold email platform like Snov" [NL 2026-02-02]. **Mass mail goes to verified business addresses only;** personal addresses are for one-to-one. Get data from Sales Navigator + LeadIQ, your LinkedIn export, or Upwork. With Upwork, have them do 50 first and review before scaling ("Don't give them ... 500 to start with").
- **Compliance isn't in the sources** (no CAN-SPAM, GDPR or SPF/DKIM). SKILL.md's hard rule applies: an honest sender identity and an opt-out where the law requires one.
- **Trigger and volume.** "Changed jobs in past 90 days" finds people "more open to hearing ... new ideas". Volume scales inversely with price. Today he'd send "20-50 heavily personalised emails per month" + "300-500 ... without heavy personalisation", adjusting monthly.
- **Fewer, more personalised.** "Sending less emails with more personalization because then you get to pick who you work with" [INT fearless-business 17:52]. Start small if you must: "one email a day, even you will start to get results" [INT fearless-business 28:14].
- **Re-email non-repliers** once the sequence ends: "another email in a month or two" [INT fearless-business 28:14].
- **Cheap laser-targeting for at-scale copy.** Add a local reference ("I'll be passing through X next week and thought…"), or echo the keyword you searched on ("We're looking to work with folks for a penchant for all things disruptive…" / "As someone that won't look at me with confused eyes when I say words like Kubernetes or..."). It reads as "they've actually looked into ... my profile".

## The Way In
- **Default angle: you're a stranger.** Poke fun at "the most obvious digital elephant in the room: the fact the prospect doesn't know me". Answer "Who are you? And what are you doing in my inbox?" before the pitch [SITE 031].
- **Never use an infomercial opener** ("Do you have trouble navigating…?"). Other tensions work too, e.g. a B2B Black-Friday email. The stranger opener is optional: his Charming Cold Open pack "used none of the devices ... And yes, those templates also work."
- **Other openers.** Lead with the news most people would hide; it "gives everything you say more credibility" [NL 2023-02-13 getting-praise]. No stranger joke? Triple Reverse: "I wanted to introduce myself in a way that showed I was interesting, witty, and clever. Alas, I wrote this email instead." [NL 2023-07-13 how-self-deprecating-humour].
- **The original drunk-email opener survives only as a fragment.** Do **not** reconstruct the rest:
  > Greetings {Firstname}, You've never heard of me. (Hi, I'm Jon.) I got your details from a list. \*gasp!\* But hey, at least you're list-worthy, that's gotta be worth something, right?

| Reaction [SITE 031] | Move |
|---|---|
| Reads it, ignores the follow-ups | Do nothing |
| Enjoyed it, "now isn't the right time" | Thank them: "May I contact you in 3/6/9 months ... I promise to include [something cute/amusing/interesting here]" |
| Meant to reply, got distracted | Automated follow-ups catch them |
| "When are you free to do a quick call?" | Book it. **Drop the irreverent tone** ("I never did"); show enthusiasm and knowledge |
| "You should be more professional!!!" | They disqualified themselves. "BONUS!" |
| Negative, polite / super mean | Unsubscribe them: "Apologies, {name}. I won't contact you again. Best of luck with everything. Have a great week, Jon." [PDF t10 p3] (swap in your name) / Don't reply |

## Magic email
**Provenance:** written by student Aaron Krall from a template in Buchan's eBook. Buchan annotated it: "you can use this template to create your own". Present it as Buchan's *template example*, not his own email. The attachment is a dog in a witch's hat and a green tutu.

```text
Bonjour {{first name}},
You've never heard of me. Hi, I'm Aaron. I got your email from top secret servers using a biorythmic
metaspyder with a 100GB nutro-plasmic VPN that I….
Ok, I got it from Clearbit.
Let me get right down to it…
I don't know if this is a problem you have, but I help software companies convert their list of
non-paying, expired trial users into paid users with ongoing email nurturing.
I'll write the emails, you send them to your list, and if you don't see more paying users you don't owe
a penny.
Yup, no high consulting fees, contracts or blood oaths.
If I don't bring in revenue, you don't owe a thing.
I'm not sure if this is a problem you have, and if it's not, that's totally cool.
If it is, would you be open to a quick 7-minute chat?
I promise to be somewhat useful, and I might even tell you my favorite joke.
Finally, it's time to release my favorite weapon.
A picture of my dog wearing a witches hat and a green tutu. Just what you've always wanted:

I await your stern but fair reply,
Aaron Krall
Free Trial Conversion Specialist / SaaS Lover / Below Average Runner
```

| Line | Slide label / job |
|---|---|
| `Bonjour` | "Unusual Greeting". A preview-pane interrupt; "all of these things add up". Buchan's default is "Greetings". |
| `top secret servers ... Clearbit.` | "Amusing honest opening line. How did you get their details?" The jargon suits a tech audience. |
| `Let me get right down to it…` | The hinge from funny to serious. |
| `I help ... ` + `blood oaths` | "Formula - I help [X] without [X]". The "without" half is a triple with the joke last. The performance offer is optional; some "risk reversal" (his term) is not: a free audit or brainstorm "you can deliver in 20 minutes ... this was key" [NL 2026-02-17]. |
| `that's totally cool` | "Just gives them an easy way out". |
| `7-minute chat` | An odd number looks "just that little bit different". |
| `somewhat useful ... favorite joke` | "Make your 'ask' - sugar coat it!" Buchan's own line is "the lamest joke I know". |
| photo · `stern but fair` · titles | "Fun ending :)" · "Anticipate negative replies" (it "stops a lot of them") · "Fun with job title" (serious / descriptive / silly) |

It works at scale with no real personalisation: it is "personal enough that it's going to get responses". "Ideas you're free to steal" is *not* in Aaron's email; it comes from Email #1 and the drunk email.

## Personalisation

| Level | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| Minimal | First name / company | Thousands of contacts |
| One true line | "I saw that your article on X, and I agree that…" | Hundreds of contacts, worth $5k+ |
| Fully one-to-one | Built around the individual | Accounts worth $10k+ |

- **Minimal works.** He used first name only for years; one true line still lifts response. Find it in Sales Navigator's "Mentioned in the news in past 30 days" (agree, quote or question), or in their bio and recommendations.
- **For one-to-one, include:** why them; that you're a customer or "a [brand name] addict", and for how long; why they beat competitors; a hint of your ideas. Close: "If you'd like to hear more, even if it's just to humour me, hit reply!"
- **Three types** [NL 2023-01-09 the-3-types]: Opportunity (an improvement you spotted), Commonality (genuine shared interest), Topicality (news, launches, press releases, which also give "a publicly available email address"). You may admit the line is there "to show your email wasn't automated" [NL 2024-09-20 whats-in-this-brand-new-pack].
- **Never fake common ground.** "If it's not genuine, don't do it." Never write "You like Kayaking? I went Kayaking once! WE SHOULD DO BUSINESS TOGETHER!"; use their article instead.

## 8-part sequence
- **8 emails, then stop. Spacing: 5 working days, in-thread.** The course said "I'll follow up once a week for eight weeks. And then I'll get the picture" [L03 33:14]; by 2026 he says "5 working days between each follow-up message", faster "is too fast", and if sending manually "reply to the last email you sent the prospect, keeping the copy from your previous emails intact" [NL 2026-02-02]. Follow-ups are short "bump[s]" [NL 2023-01-13]. The "+1 week" labels below are course history. **(unverified — legacy):** "steps a few days apart" and "Email #2 a few hours/1 day later".
- **Every email must give value and/or entertain,** with a different benefit each time. Never nag ("You didn't reply to my email I sent 2 days ago. Are you interested?"), and never revert to bland copy.
- **Architectures** [PDF L04 p28–29]:
  - **A. Spread Out The Sales Pitch** (feature-rich or corporate offers): intro → benefit 1 → against / pains avoided → benefit 2 → case study → against → benefit 3 → breakup.
  - **B. Funny Each Time** (simple offers): intro → six funny follow-ups → funny breakup.
  - The template below is mostly B, with #4 as the value email. For A, rewrite #2–#7 from the sender's benefits and "against" list.

**Email #1 — Introduction** (week 1). The Magic email, generalised. `{honest/funny reason here}` is The Way In. "Even the slightest bit curious" sets a very low bar. "Ideas you're free to steal" makes the call a gift.
```text
Greetings {{first name}},
You've never heard of me. Hi, I'm {firstname}. I got your email from {honest/funny reason here}.
Let me get right down to it…
I don't know if this is a problem you have, but I help {solution to problem}
I {what you do}
If you’re even the slightest bit curious, would you be open to a quick 7-minute chat?
I promise to give you some ideas you’re free to steal. If you play your cards right, I might even tell
you the lamest joke I know!
Finally, it's time to release my favorite weapon.
A picture of my {pet name} {doing something cute} Just what you've always wanted:

I await your profanity-filled response.
Have an absolutely terrific day,
First Name Surname
{job title one} / {job title two} / {job title three - funniest}

(Remember to insert picture)
```
**Email #2 — "Forgot to mention"** (the template gives no timing; default to week 2). The stage directions (first name only, "Sent from my iPhone") are his, to make it read as a personal note. **"(No Unsubscribe Link)" is overridden by SKILL.md:** keep an opt-out where anti-spam law requires one, and flag this to the user.
```text
Sorry, forgot to mention - you can find more information at our website (www.url.com ) - I'll get back
to nervously anticipating your response.

First Name

    -    Sent from my iPhone

(No Unsubscribe Link)
```

## Direct mail
- **Why post:** it reaches hard-to-reach and local prospects, and has "a lot less competition". Boring post still gets binned. Postage pays for big accounts. 2025: "Sending physical letters is unusual again, which makes them more effective than ever" [NL 2025-07-12 last-chance]. New formats (copy mostly sold): a £0 invoice ("Payment terms: A quick call.") [NL 2025-07-09 polite-bribe], a faux cease & desist [NL 2025-07-19 the-faux-cease-desist], a WANTED poster, a future newspaper (knowledge/03 §7.7).
- **The combo, his "favourite weapon"** [PDF L04 p33]:
  1. Send a letter with "something unusual in it" (the ferret sticker).
  2. A few days later, send an email naming that oddity **in the subject line**.
  3. Make the email "different to the letter but in a similar style", never a copy. "You've now made a great impression twice."
  Opens were "as high as 70%" (decks) and "80%+" for "Sorry for the ferret in the post". Both figures are his.
- **Getting it opened.** Letter: typewriter font, **no logo, no images**, just your address and good copy; odd or thick paper helps. Envelope: bright and hand-addressed. Optional: a wax seal, a handwritten letter, or a postcard (no sensitive info).
- **Handwritten-letter formula** [SITE 010]: **[Attention]** the form → **[Disarm]** "Open your letter with the most honest thing you can say." → **[Communicate]** "Be understated but ambitious. Meek but direct." → **[Charm]** they "stand to gain". No reply? Follow up "one week later, with the subject line 'handwritten letter.'"
- **Third touch: warm call.** "I'm the one who sent you [X]". It works on gatekeepers too. He rarely needed it.

**Follow-up email, subject "My unhinged letter".** OCR-corrected; "cut-through" is struck through in the original. The subject names the oddity, not the pitch. "If you didn't get the letter..." covers lost post.
```text
Greetings ,
I thought it was a good idea to send you a rather ugly letter in the post. My apologies :(
To make up for such a crime against typography, I've included picture of me rescuing a baby and a basket of kittens from a burning building.
I hope this shows I'm not all bad...
[photo: Jon photoshopped walking out of a burning house holding a baby, a second Jon carrying a basket of kittens]
If you didn't get the letter... this email must seem very odd. Do let me know if you didn't and I can explain myself!
If you're feeling curious or daring or you just wish to procrastinate, I'd relish the chance to chat to you about creating sales and marketing campaigns that ~~cut-through~~ the noise. Fingers crossed...
Have an absolutely wonderful weekend!
Best Wishes,
Jon Buchan
Chief of Staff
```
**Follow-up email, subject "Cold Call Bingo Card".** The letter held a jargon-bingo card: "that can only be one person". The structure is a triple, then understatement ("somewhat", "of varying quality"), then a 7-minute fallback.
```text
Greetings ,
This email may or may not make any sense, but please bear with me...
I thought it was a wise idea to send you a letter in the post containing a 'cold call bingo card'. If you didn't receive it, your crafty postman or one of your colleagues may be in possession of it. Dun dun dunn!
To recap, I create compelling direct mail, email and social media campaigns that cut-through the noise.
At this point, my attempt to get a meeting with you is either going reasonably well or pretty terribly.
If you're feeling daring and would like to meet up, I will buy you lunch/coffee/tequila shots and promise to be somewhat entertaining. If you're lucky, I may even wear a top hat.
First off, I'm happy to listen to your (sales/marketing) problems and provide you with some ideas of varying quality you are free to steal. If you're too busy to meet, I would love get just 7 minutes on the phone with you.
I've attached a photo of my most recent bout of bravery. I trust this will charm you into submission.
[same burning-building photo]
I await your profanity filled response.
Best Wishes,
Jon Buchan
Director & Interim Head of Stationery
CHARM OFFENSIVE
```

## Subject lines
- **Exploit being a stranger.** Be unusual: they see only your name, the subject and a line or two.
- **In the combo, name the letter's oddity.** For corporates who won't go "crazy", reference a passion or an article they wrote, but don't tame the body ("the more I tamed stuff down, the worse my responses were").
- **Real examples:** "apologies in advance" (email 1) · "fortune favours the brave" · "fingers crossed" · "Sorry for the ferret in the post" · "My unhinged letter" · "Cold Call Bingo Card" · "handwritten letter".
- **No fake "RE:"** ("it uses deception") unless the first lines immediately joke about it [NL 2023-08-17 re-fake-subject-line]. Personalise with company, industry, competitor or result [NL 2024-10-01 personalising-your-cold-pitch]. Follow-ups: "you call this a follow up attempt?" / "are you giving me the secret signal to shut up?" [NL 2024-10-03 subject-lines-to-people]; Grief: "oh my... no reply :(" / "I messed up..." / "ok... you got me, {firstname}." [NL 2026-02-02].
- **"The drunk cold email that made me [one million] in revenue"**: sourced as a podcast-pitch subject line [L06 4:08]; **(unverified — legacy)** as a cold-email subject. Also unverified: "never 'Quick question' / 'Touching base'". Newsletter open-rate subjects are not cold rules.

## Props, images and dream clients
- **Plain text beats designed.** Buchan told HP to swap the designed event invite for plain, informal text: events that drew "zero people" were then filled [INT eofire 15:44]. Don't dress up the email.
- **Open-rate baselines (his own).** The original email ran at "like 40% open rates"; the follow-up after the ferret letter got "80% open rates" [INT eofire 26:11]. Don't quote these as guarantees.
- **Close on a pet or absurd photo,** e.g. Colin the ferret in bunny ears, or Jon rescuing a baby and kittens from a burning building.
- **Niche images:** comic strips aim for a smirk (e.g. "we hacked it" for cyber sales directors). Show the skill in the pitch (a designer's comic, an accountant's fake pie chart, "When threatened at gunpoint"). Niftyimages can put the prospect's name in the image.
- **Format parodies** (a press release) only work if the audience knows the format. Fake products can mock their pain (Cold Call Bingo, a custom wine label from two interests).
- **Dream clients: "do something special".** Research their socials and have a designer make something just for them: an illustration, a poster, a "certificate", a book with a handwritten note. Then follow up. ("maybe sort of 40, 50 quid"; the ego rub gets shared.)
- **Corporates are people.** He won meetings at Red Bull, HP and Symantec untamed.
- **"Job titleist."** Don't write to the title; IT directors filled HP's event once the invite turned plain and informal [INT eofire 15:44] [INT eofire 17:47].

## Mistakes to avoid
- **Opening wrong:** going straight to persuasion, skipping the stranger question, infomercial openers, bland "best practice" templates, or cramming every feature into email 1.
- **Tone and data:** taming the copy for corporates, faking common ground, or mass-mailing personal or unverified addresses.
- **Follow-ups:** nagging, or not following up at all.
- **Format:** unrecognised formats, logo-heavy letters, or jokes with no content.
- **Reusing one famous template** on heavily prospected execs: "the charm offensive thing is more about an approach", not lines [INT modern-sales-pt1 19:00]. *(Our inference: vary the lines.)*
- **Process:** quitting after one negative reply ("Don't get upset at words on the internet"), perfectionism, or staying irreverent after a keen reply.

## Applied guardrails (ours, from eval review, not Buchan rules)
- Length: **120–200 words** of body is *our* first-touch default (the magic email sits there). Buchan's own rule is boredom, not length: "You can never be too long but you can be too boring" [NL 2025-12-22 my-recent-talk-at-the-london-school], though he conceded "there are advantages to being concise when writing humour based copy" [NL 2024-09-18 creativity-thrives]. Run longer only if every extra line entertains; if it can't, keep it short.
- **Every prop must pay something off.** The photo or attachment should call back something set up earlier in the email, not be random.
- Use one or two personal facts about the prospect, not all of them. Stacking facts reads as surveillance, not charm.

## Drafting checklist
1. **Mode** (Magic / sequence A or B / letter + email) and **personalisation level** match the account value; plain text, not designed.
2. **Opening:** an unusual greeting plus an honest, amusing Way In that answers "Who are you? And what are you doing in my inbox?"
3. **Offer:** one line of "I help [X] without [Y]", after a "Let me get right down to it…" hinge.
4. **Ask:** only a short call (7 minutes), sugar-coated ("ideas you're free to steal", "somewhat"), with an easy out ("that's totally cool").
5. **Ending:** a fun ending (photo or prop named), a formal-assumptive closer, and three titles with the funniest last.
6. **Sequence:** each email brings a different benefit or a fresh gag, ≥5 working days apart and in-thread (his 2026 rule; the course said a week), with the breakup at #8. No nagging.
7. **Letter combo:** the subject names the letter's oddity; the email is new copy in the same style and covers "if you didn't get the letter".
8. **Compliance:** every fact, compliment and stat is genuine, and an opt-out is present where the law requires it (flag Email #2). Prefer a Buchan-voiced opt-out (banks.md "Opt-out lines"; e.g. "click this link as hard as you can" [NL 2026-02-02]).
9. **Read aloud:** funny/serious/funny, no repeated formula, no jargon or hype.
