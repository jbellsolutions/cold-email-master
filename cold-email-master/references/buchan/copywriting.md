# Copywriting beyond 1:1 outreach (runtime reference)

Modes: `copy-sales-letter` / `copy-landing-page`, `copy-linkedin-post` / `copy-social-post`, `copy-newsletter`, `copy-bio`.
Joke mechanics: voice-and-humour.md. ISDS story and offer: story-and-offer.md. Line banks (buttons, deadline lines, sign-offs, opt-outs): banks.md §6, §8, §11, §16, "Opt-out lines". Profile basics: linkedin.md §Profile, §Content.

## Shared rules (all four modes)
- **The context changes the copy.** Cold humour "is based around the fact the prospect has no idea who you are"; "When the context changes, so must the copy." [NL 2023-03-17]. On launches: his style transfers, "but with modifications" [RES pro_course]. Don't paste cold templates onto a page.
- **The length rule.** "Be funny. If you can't be funny, be clever. If you can't be clever, keep that shit short." Copy "can never be too long, just too boring" [NL 2025-06-08] [NL 2024-09-18]. Long pages are fine only if every section entertains or informs.
- **Humour is spice.** Too much and "people won't know whether to take you seriously or not" [NL 2024-03-16]. Keep a funny → serious rhythm, with a laugh before each ask [L03 32:07].
- **Every successful approach tells a story** ("The Ambitious Underdog") [PDF L09 p33]. Stories "show off your expertise, character, and positive attributes ... in a format people will actually read in full" [SITE 098].
- **Say the awkward thing first** (the Buffett technique): the worst news gives "everything else ... more credibility" [INT reddit]. On a page, that means confessing the unflattering origin, the self-interest or the genre's tricks.
- **State your self-interest openly** ("radical candour") [NL 2025-12-20]. He teaches "talking up your own self-interested goals to great persuasive effect" [RES pro_course] (pack description).
- **Repel on purpose.** Write copy that attracts "clients who enjoy reading it - while simultaneously repelling those with poor taste" [SITE 102]. That is "a useful and deliberate feature" [SITE 034].
- **Never use hype or jargon** [SITE 010] [PDF L09 p33]. Parody hype; don't use it sincerely (H3).
- **Proof is real.** Use screenshots, names across sectors and third-party posts [PDF Reddit p7–8]. Credit the team: "team effort and it would be wrong to assert otherwise" [NL 2023-09-27].
- **Profanity** ("cockwomble", tier names, "keep that shit short") lives on his own pages and list. Keep client copy clean unless the sender's own audience already talks like that **(applied)**.

## copy-sales-letter / copy-landing-page

#### Four skeletons from his own pages (pick one; beats in order)
**A. Origin-story pack letter** [SITE 072] [SITE 075] [SITE 081]
1. A qualifying question, then "If so, read on."
2. ISDS in one-line beats.
3. Screenshots, with a self-deprecating aside.
4. Big names across sectors, then a second proof story (VentureBeat).
5. Other uses ("I've used it to…" ×3), then students' results.
6. "You can get results like these", then "Introducing [X]" plus a one-line belief.
7. A ✓ checklist of the situations it covers, then bonuses with values.
8. The price staircase, then the button.

**B. Pack launch page** [SITE 104] [SITE 115] [SITE 117]
1. A "*NEW*" headline plus a one-line scope, then "Special Launch Offer!"
2. The problem, quoting the bad version the reader recognises ("You didn't reply to my email I sent 2 days ago. Are you interested?").
3. A rhetorical question with a self-aware answer, then "Enter The [X]."
4. "Who Is This For?" in three bullets, then contents as situation bullets with funny adjectives.
5. Bonuses, then "Does This Stuff Work? Ohhhh YES It Does:" over the screenshots.
6. The dated offer, then the FAQ.

**C. The anti-sales letter** [SITE 064] [SITE 102]
1. A WARNING / classified-ad headline that admits the motive.
2. Name the genre's tricks and refuse them ("I can't write hypey copy").
3. "Ok. Enough frivolity and prevaricating for a moment..." then what it is.
4. The "WARNING: Do not buy this product if:" / "You should probably buy this product if:" lists.
5. Contents, with one joke per module and serious detail inside.
6. A reader-voice interruption to reach the price, then tiers with funny names.
7. Bonuses with a joke item, the guarantee, proof, and a callback close.

**D. Opt-in / ad landing page** [SITE 055] [SITE 058] [RES free_mini_course] [RES jon_buchan_home]
1. Thank them for the click, with its cost visible.
2. "an offer you can refuse", a free brainstorm, a pet photo and a joke bribe.
3. A button parody, then "How do I know this is legit?"
4. Proof, the button again, then a deliverability note.

#### Carrying a long page (humour + honesty + story)
- **Narrate your own page.** "You forgot I’d started a list, didn't you? (I had too…)" [SITE 102]. "If you couldn’t, this sales page would end here - but as you can see, there’s more copy to come." [SITE 115].
- **Put words in the reader's mouth** to move to the next section: "“WHEN ARE YOU GOING TO GET TO THE PRICE, JON?!? HURRY UP! I HAVEN'T GOT ALL FAFFING DAY.” / Alright, pottymouth. / As you wish..." [SITE 064]. Another: "Would you like that? / "yeah, I guess so" I hear you SCREAM!" [SITE 064].
- **Parody the genre's furniture.** "What self-respecting long-ass sales letter doesn’t contain a plethora of free bonuses?" [SITE 102]. "*WHILE STOCKS LAST*" sits on a free brainstorm [SITE 102].
- **A joke in the header, substance underneath.** Each FACT or Module opens with a gag, then gives real detail (skills, client range, what's taught) [SITE 102] [SITE 064].
- **Use one-line beats, ✓ checklists and blank lines.** Write bullets that "evoke curiosity with their combination of vagueness and specificity" [RES pro_course] (pack description).
- **Close the loop.** A callback ending: "If only I were competent enough to write a good callback joke." [SITE 064]. Or step out of character and thank them [SITE 102].

#### Proof
- **Lead with the claim, then the evidence:** "Take a look at these screenshots." Add a self-aware aside: "There's more where that came from. I've saved them all. This might show my insecurity somewhat!" [SITE 072].
- **Use real replies as set-up/punch,** e.g. "My colleague forwarded me your spam email…" then "This is completely true by the way!" [PDF Reddit p6].
- **Point at outside verification,** such as LinkedIn Recommendations "date all the way back to 2007" [RES free_mini_course].
- **Testimonial gags.** His "Can you figure out which one it is? Technically, the fake one is still true. :D" [SITE 102] is allowed only if every quote is real (a mum, a pet). A fabricated client quote is H1 **(applied)**.

#### Objection handling
- **Acknowledge their fears, then "quash them mercilessly"** [SITE 024].
- **Answer the legitimacy question head-on:** "How do I know this is legit? / First of all, you are right to be cautious. The internet harbours many a charlatan. However, I am no such cowboy." [RES free_mini_course].
- **The FAQ is objection handling with jokes.** "What niches do they work in? / Any. They are not niche specific. If you're targeting humans, these templates will work. If you're not, you have other problems. 😀" [SITE 104]. Also answer "Do I have these templates?!" and "How do I use these templates?" honestly, e.g. his "I personally would leave a week between follow-up messages out of common courtesy." [SITE 115].
- **Disqualify out loud.** "Warning! Do not buy this product, if:" then "But you should probably buy this product, if:" [NL 2024-08-31] [SITE 064].

#### Offer, price, guarantee, CTA
- **Price staircase:** "How much? / $150? / Nope! / $125? / Even better than that! / … the patently absurd price of just $99." [SITE 072].
- **Funny tier names:** "JUST THE TIPS … For toe-dippers with a fear of missing out" and "HELPING HAND … "I'm not a cheapskate. I'll buy the middle level package!"" [SITE 064].
- **Bonus stacks may carry one joke item:** "A Warm, Fuzzy Feeling For Helping Me Make A Living Doing What I Love … ( Valued at: PRICELESS / $0)" [SITE 064].
- **Guarantee with a joke:**
  ```text
  30 Day Better Than Money Back Guarantee
  Get your money back within 30 days of purchase with any reason [sic]. No questions asked. Other than 'Would you please reconsider? Go oonnnn..."
  Ok. Ok. I won't even ask that question.
  ```
  [SITE 064]. His plain versions: "14 day money back guarantee" [SITE 053]; "Subscribe now to lock in current pricing forever. Cancel any time." [RES pro_course].
- **Risk reversal for service pages.** Offer a free brainstorm or audit, which "reverses the risk"; "this was key" [SITE 055] [NL 2026-02-17]. Anticipate the fear "would look flashy, cost a lot, yet deliver no results" [SITE 024].
- **Deadlines and scarcity.** His pages use real, dated ones: "This offer expires midnight (UK time), 11th September." [SITE 104]. He teaches making scarcity "far more fun" [RES pro_course] (pack description). Never add urgency the offer doesn't have. "AT LEAST 10x", "VERY LIMITED" and countdowns are used only when literally true, and always alongside an exit ("Never fear, this is by no means mandatory." [SITE 055]) **(applied; CLAUDE.md low-pressure rule)**.
- **CTA and button copy** (more in banks.md §6):
  - "If nothing else, you'll get some ideas you're free to steal." [SITE 055]
  - "please click the button below with all your enthusiasm. / No flimsy clicks please!" [SITE 055]
  - "click the magenta button with all the enthusiasm you can muster." [RES jon_buchan_home]
  - Button labels: "HELP ME, MASTER DUCHAN. OH, DO BESTOW UPON ME YOUR PROMOTIONAL WIZADRY [sic]" [SITE 102]; "GO ON THEN, LET'S DO THIS!" [SITE 055].
- **The sign-off and P.S. A long page ends like a letter:** "Thank you for reading. / Have an absolutely splendid day, / Joe" [SITE 102]. A pet-photo close ("Please find enclosed a picture of a cat begging. / His name is Rufus.") [SITE 055]. A practical P.S. on opt-in pages: "If the email hasn't arrived in the next 10 minutes, check your promotions folder, and failing that, your spam folder." [RES free_mini_course].
- **Thank-you and unsubscribe pages stay in voice:** "YOUR MAJESTIC & MYSTERIOUS B2B SALES MINI GUIDE IS EN ROUTE TO YOUR INBOX!" [SITE 058]; "Cajolement ceased! … YOU WIN THIS ROUND! 😀" [SITE 107].

## copy-linkedin-post / copy-social-post

- **Job:** be interesting, tell stories, "Don't pitch all the time!" If you pitch, "hide it with humour", e.g. a meme [PDF L05 p22] [L05 17:43] [NL 2024-10-27]. Story posts are among his most engaged [SITE 098].
- **Post types he shows:**
  - speaking "the truth" / going against convention;
  - a succinct story;
  - making a point with humour (a mock product launch).
  - Group posts add method posts, `[BUSINESS TALK]` stories, engagement posts and opt-in posts [PDF L05 p23–25] [PDF L09 p32] [PDF L09 p50].
- **Hook = the first line.** An accidental-origin or oxymoron title ("The drunk cold email that changed my life."), plus a smirk "within the first 10 seconds" [PDF Reddit p4] [PDF Reddit p12]. "This formula is likely reusable." [PDF Reddit p12].
- **"No walls of text!"** One line, then a space. On LinkedIn, "you've got to be quite succinct" [PDF L05 p23] [L05 15:39].
- **The link goes in the first comment,** never the body ("LinkedIn restricts your reach"). Close with "If you'd like to grab a copy, check the link in the comments." [PDF L05 p24].
- **Engagement.** Seed early likes (friends, group, list, "Commenting for reach") [L05 18:43] [NL 2026-01-16]. Reply to every comment "obsessively" [PDF Reddit p11]. Bridge to a DM: "Thank you [Name], I'll send you a DM now!" [NL 2024-11-23] (FB ads; LinkedIn use **applied**). Write at least one line readers will quote back [NL 2024-03-16].
- **Reddit / long-form story.** Provide "tons of useful content. Go overboard!" (he included the template), which earns "a pass to post a URL at the very end". Cover "multiple sectors, company sizes and uses" so nobody says "I bet it won't work for XXXX". Use proof posted by someone else [PDF Reddit p8] [PDF Reddit p12].

#### The drunk-email post structure (LinkedIn version [PDF L09 p19]; Reddit [PDF Reddit p3])
1. **Title line:** an accidental, life-changing claim.
2. **One line on why you're different:** "I never learned how I was 'meant' to write copy, so I interrupted the pattern by default."
3. **A personal aside:** "From the age of 7, I was obsessed with stand up comedy…"
4. **Struggle:** "all my word of mouth work had dried up. I was desperate for sales."
5. **Discovery:** "One night, I got rather drunk and wrote the most absurd cold email I could muster."
6. **Surprise + proof:** "To my astonishment, it worked." Then brands across sectors.
7. **Optional, for Reddit:** the real-quote set-up/punch, other uses, an offer of help.
8. **CTA:** "If you'd like to grab a copy, check the link in the comments."

## copy-newsletter (a list the sender owns)

- **Register.** He writes to opted-in readers: warm, chatty, repetitive, sometimes profane. None of that goes into cold copy [09c §0]. The welcome autoresponder was long teaching stories [NL 2021-10-02].
- **Teaching emails.** A ✅ subject marker, a one-idea lesson in short lines, and often a "Breaking news:" turn. A soft pitch at the end is fenced off as an aside [NL 2025-12-16]:
  ```text
  *(psssst: I have a cheeky little offer on my Charming Lead Gen Pack. …
  If you're wondering "Do I have this pack already, Jon?" The answer is "no". I've segmented out existing … customers.)*
  ```
  When the teaching run ends, flag the pitch: "Ok. Time for the blatant pitch I promised you." [NL 2021-10-11].
- **Promo emails** are radical candour plus benefit bullets plus a link with gusto:
  - "In the interests of radical candour: This email is an attempt to separate you from some of your money." [NL 2025-12-20]
  - "This is not entirely a generous act. … I would be lying by omission if I didn't admit that I also want to generate a nice bit of dosh" [NL 2025-12-18]
  - "the money‑extraction portal can be found by clicking the lovely pink link below" [NL 2025-12-20]
- **Sale sequence** (observed Dec 2025, order **applied**):
  1. Launch ("✅ NEW: …").
  2. One objection or FAQ per email ("Who is the FB Ads -> Direct To Offer Pack for?", "What budget do you need…", "What does … cover").
  3. A results email.
  4. A "long email 📜".
  5. Last-chance emails ("Last shameless poke: 1 hour left": "In about an hour, the frankly daft price … disappears.") [NL 2025-12-10…22].
  
  Near the close he sends several a day. State the real deadline plainly ("you have until midnight tomorrow") [NL 2025-12-18].
- **Opt-out-from-this-sale P.S. on every sale email.** It is per offer, apologetic and often self-roasting: "Not interested in this sale? Click here to opt out of further emails about it. Much as that saddens me..." [NL 2022-12-07]. In 2023 he skipped it on "last chance" [NL 2023-06-18]; by Dec 2025 even the 1-hour email carries it [NL 2025-12-22], so always include it (recency rule). More lines: banks.md "Opt-out lines"; 09c §5.
- **Subject lines.** Use curiosity, confession ("I screwed up. Apologies."), absurd, lowercase or stretched-sound subjects ("Laaaaast orrrdderrrs"). One or two emoji as markers [09c §1]. Never a fake "RE:" [NL 2023-08-17].
- **Opening lines** are short and in-character: "Me again.", "Ahem.", "I'll be blunt.", "Let me level with you...", "Most sales messages are like a clingy stranger at a party. Too eager. Too familiar. Too much." [09c §2].
- **Sign-offs:** "Cheers," (half of all sends) or "Have an absolutely wonderful / splendid / lovely day,". Occasionally "Stay vigilant" or "Stay outta trouble". Then just the first name [09c §3].
- **P.S.** Use it for one extra smile or reason, e.g. a teaser for tomorrow's email, a "rather dapper goat", proof, or a joke coupon. Separate from the opt-out P.S. [09c §4].
- **Exit page:** "Cajolement ceased!" [SITE 107].

## copy-bio (LinkedIn About, website About, speaker bio)

- **The headline formula:** "I help [who] [outcome], [outcome] and [outcome]". His: "I help founders stand out, book more sales calls and build their influence online." [PDF L02 p4]. Make it "intriguing, interesting, and relevant", because the profile "acts as a sales page" [NL 2026-02-17]. An optional emoji after the name [L02 2:04].
- **The About section:** Identity (light) → Struggle → Discovery → Surprise → what you do now → link → CTA. Use an informal tone, "Refreshing honesty is welcome", and no jargon [PDF L02 p5–6] [L02 13:33]. His CTA: "if you need your message to cut through the noise, you should get in touch" [L02 4:12]. If the story doesn't fit ISDS, "don't worry too much" [L02 4:12].
- **Alternative hook:** "Greetings / I have a weird skill / I can write emails and letters that open opportunities." Then proof, then "Check out the group – or ping me a message" [PDF L09 p18].
- **Website About** [RES jon_buchan_home]:
  1. A story headline ("The Drunk Cold Email That Changed My Life").
  2. ISDS with a real quote.
  3. The honest reason it worked: "I interrupted the pattern by virtue of not knowing what the “best practice guidelines” were."
  4. "The Birth of [X]" and what you teach now.
  5. An opt-in CTA "with all the enthusiasm you can muster".
- **Smile lines:** honest small print ("Pretty Mediocre GCSEs" [PDF L02 p11]) and a three-title line (serious / descriptive / silly) [PDF L03 p6]. Readers quote a line that made them smile, so plant one [NL 2024-03-16].
- **Speaker bio (applied; not in sources):** 3–5 sentences in the third person. Compress ISDS to two sentences, add the current role and one real proof point (a stage, brand or result), and end on one silly true detail. It borrows his speaking-pitch logic: a "compelling story" that makes the talk worth hearing [PDF L08 p14].

## Drafting checklist

**Rubric map (rubric.md; H1–H6 always apply, H7–H9 never).** For copy, read S1 as the opening/hook, S10 as audience- or segment-specific (not prospect-specific), and S7 as the exit (the "by no means mandatory" line or the opt-out P.S.). A real deadline with an exit is not H5. Invented scarcity is H1.

Use the threshold from rubric.md (≥75% of applicable).

**copy-sales-letter / copy-landing-page**
1. Name the skeleton (A/B/C/D). The opening confesses something (origin, motive or genre) within three lines.
2. The story beats are real. Proof sits right after the claim, across sectors, with no invented testimonial.
3. Each section has a gag up top and substance inside. Read it aloud: no joke-only stretches.
4. Objections are handled (FAQ, legit question, not-for-you list). The offer has a guarantee or risk reversal if the sender has one.
5. Price, deadline and scarcity are true. Every CTA has an exit. The button copy is in voice.
6. It ends on a sign-off, callback or pet. The thank-you and opt-out pages stay in voice.

**copy-linkedin-post / copy-social-post**
1. The first line hooks (oxymoron, accident, "truth"), and there's a smirk within 10 seconds.
2. One line per paragraph. No walls of text.
3. It's a story, a truth or a humorous point. Any pitch is hidden in humour or a meme.
4. The link is in the first comment, and the post says so. There's a plan to reply to every comment.
5. For Reddit, the value comes first, overboard, with the URL only at the end.

**copy-newsletter**
1. The type is set (teaching / promo / FAQ / last chance), and the subject line fits a list (no fake RE:).
2. The opening line is in character. Self-interest is admitted on promos.
3. Teaching emails fence the pitch off as an aside. Promos use benefit bullets and a link with gusto.
4. The deadline is real. An opt-out-from-this-offer P.S. is on every sale email.
5. Use a Cheers / "Have an absolutely …" sign-off, plus a P.S. for one extra smile.

**copy-bio**
1. The headline is "I help [who] [outcome]…" and is intriguing but clear.
2. ISDS (Identity light) → now → link → CTA.
3. There's at least one honest smile line. No jargon, no invented credentials.
4. Mark the speaker bio as applied. Keep it to ≤5 sentences, with one real proof point.
