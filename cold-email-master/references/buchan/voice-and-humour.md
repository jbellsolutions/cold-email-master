# Voice & Humour (runtime reference)

Quoted lines are Buchan's. Lines marked *(inference)* are ours, not his.

## 1. What the copy has to do

- **Impact → communicate → persuade.** Most cold approaches fail because they "go straight to persuasion" [SITE 034].
- **The job is small.** Sell the idea that a call "is not a terrible use of ... their time", not the whole offer [L03 21:50].
- **Any reaction beats indifference.** "You only need a smirk to get a positive response" [SITE 031]. The B2B bar is "(let's be charitable here) ... low".
- **Talk to people, not pain points.** "Pain points vary. Markets often share little in common. But people do" [SITE 008].
- **Humour is reliable, not mandatory.** "not ... the only approach or the 'best' approach". Sales letters still need pain points.
- **Humour amplifies; it can't rescue.** "Humor potentiates an already persuasive message" but won't make people take an offer that "is not relevant" [INT fearless-business 23:03]. "Without the right offer, all of the other stuff won't matter" [INT selzy]. Fix the offer before the jokes.
- **The skeleton** [SITE 010]:
  1. **Attention.**
  2. **Disarm:** the most honest thing first.
  3. **Communicate:** what you do, "in a way that will make people smile".
  4. **Charm:** the ask, framed as their gain.

## 2. Voice rules

0. **His three-tier rule: "Be funny. If you can't be funny, be clever. If you can't be clever, keep that shit short."** [RES pro_course] [NL 2025-06-08 freebie-alert-clever]. "Most people can't do 1 or 2, so they go straight to 3." Copy "can never be too long, just too boring" [NL 2024-09-18 creativity-thrives].
   - **Humour is spice, not the main ingredient.** "It should be used as a spice rather than the main ingredient. If you add too much, people won't know whether to take you seriously or not. It also gets too predictable and thus, less funny." [NL 2024-03-16 questions-and-helpfully-answers] (from his AI-pack email; it applies to AI-drafted and hand-written copy alike).
1. **Write to the person, not the title.** "Most people are people first, professionals second." Respect them, but don't put them on a pedestal.
2. **Disarming honesty.** Say the awkward thing first: where you got their email, that you're nervous, that this is a pitch. Admitting the list is "refreshing honesty" [L03 2:04].
   - **The Warren Buffett technique:** open with your worst or most honest news, so "everything else he has to say has more credibility" [INT reddit] [INT yt-cold-email-opener 1:02]. If a joke doesn't fit, be "more honest than is expected" [INT yt-drunk-cold-email-changed-life 17:10].
3. **Self-deprecate,** but about your situation, never your skills [INT selzy]. It's the safe default target.
4. **Understated but ambitious. Meek but direct.** No hype [SITE 010].
5. **Frame the ask as their gain.** "Ideas you're free to steal". You may openly admit "It is of course, a thinly veiled sales pitch" (labelled "Disarming honesty") [PDF L05 p20].
6. **The five mistakes** [PDF L09 p33]:
   - (1) full of ego
   - (2) trying to impress with design or persuade with jargon
   - (3) rhythm and cadence that isn't an enjoyable read
   - (4) presumptions
   - (5) "They play it safe! (This is huge!)"

   The aim is to break the "us and them" dynamic.
7. **Write to one person, the way people talk.** No "cheesy 80s infomercial voice" ("Do you have trouble navigating…?").
8. **Tell a story.** His is "The Ambitious Underdog". See story-and-offer.md.
9. **Be daring.** Playing it safe is the big mistake, and so is "being too hesitant" [PDF L09 p33] [SITE 012].
10. **Genuine only.** "If you think something isn't genuine, don't include it" [PDF L01 p10].
11. **Make the meeting feel fun and safe.** They get "free ideas, and ... a good time" [L03 22:51].
12. **Any register works.** Blunt, dry, witty or silly: "there are structures you can use and they will work no matter what your style" [INT modern-sales-pt1 14:50]. Match the user's register; don't clone Buchan's.

## 3. Humour formulas

Buchan's caveat: these come from stand-up, so you can't control the emphasis on the page. "At worst, they can help make your writing more punchy" [PDF L09 p43].

**Write humour from named formulas, not free-form "jokes".** AI's free jokes "can be pretty lame", but "for syntax-based humour, ChatGPT is pretty good" [INT selzy]. Pick a formula from this table and fill its slots. "Some of ChatGPT's versions of my lines are better than my original ones" [NL 2023-06-22 hyperbole-backpedal], but its output is never his copy, and AI images must not "obviously look like AI" [NL 2026-02-02].

| Formula | Structure | Buchan's example | Use / limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reverse** (his favourite) | [CREATE MENTAL IMAGE] → [SHATTER IT] | "I got your contact details from a list. \*gasp!\* but hey at least you're list-worthy, that's gotta be worth something right?!" | Best as an opener that turns an awkward truth into a compliment. Write the story, list Who/What/Where/Why/When/How, remove elements, image first and shatter second, cut it down ("Don't blabber"), then test it on people. |
| **Triple** (newsletter name: Comic Triple, "1. Normal 2. Normal 3. What?!" [NL 2023-04-11 comic-triple]) | [SENSIBLE] - [SENSIBLE] - [SILLY] | "coffee / lunch / tequila shots… and promise to be somewhat entertaining" · "phone, through email or by deploying carrier pigeons" | **Once per email at most.** Repeated, "it will just look weird". The set-up can be longer. Read it out loud. |
| **List** | Long boring list, absurd last item | "copywriting, social media consultancy, video production, analytics audits and bedroom tidying" | Call-out variant: number the services, bury the absurd one, then add "One of these isn't true. I'll leave you to decide which that is." The once-per-email limit applies, since a list is a triple extension. |
| **Triple Reverse** | Triple sets up, reverse knocks down | "I'd like to introduce myself in a way that shows I'm interesting, witty and clever. Alas, I wrote this email instead." | Works for LinkedIn invites, emails, "all sorts of things". Podcast variant: "succinct, clever and witty". |
| **Understatement / litotes** | Claim less than you mean | "It'll be the best thing you've done all hour." · "The client wasn't displeased with the results we achieved." | Avoids arrogance. Optional, "something you can play with". |
| **Exaggeration + walk-back** | Clearly over the top, then honestly retracted | "contracts so restrictive they come with a set of complementary handcuffs" · "those evil (ok not evil... but not as good) other agencies" | Best on pain points. It must be obviously ridiculous. |
| **Formal & Assumptive → Surprise** | [Formal/assumptive] – [unexpected] | "I trust this will charm you into submission." · "I await your profanity-filled response." | Mostly the closing line. A daring word is fine when it "makes sense within the context". You can generalise it: parody a document form, e.g. a press release. |
| **Funny words** | One odd word in a stock phrase | "I await your baffled response with great interest." Bank: bamboozled, hoodwinked, flummoxed, tomfoolery, horseplay, antics, shenanigans, frolics, besmirched | **Sparingly**. They're hard to be offended by. |
| **Oxymoron** | Contradictory pair | "Rather predictably, I'd like to have a phone call with you ... random trivia about my hometown that you'll find borderline fascinating." | Mainly for softening a request. |
| **Parody** | Fake product or ad that mocks a shared pain | Auto Brand Police ("stop daring ideas before they catch on"), Agency Bullshit Guard, "Introducing..... Direct Mail!" | Aim it at a shared enemy or yourself. For the FDs, the target "was actually myself". |
| **Meme** (extra) | Image carries the premise, text carries the punch | — | Only if the audience will get the reference. |
| **Antithesis / tricolon** (punch, not jokes) | Parallel contrast, or three beats with repetition | "We need to work hard. We need to stay determined. We need to remain focused." | "Don't try and force it". |

## 4. Sugar-coated requests (the main job of the humour)

"You can disguise these and make them more palatable using humour" [PDF L03 p21]. Examples:
- "If you play [your] cards right, I'll sweeten the deal by telling you the lamest joke I know."
- "Rather predictably, I'd like to have a call with you. If you agree to this, I'll even tell you some random trivia that you will find borderline fascinating."

**Three options CTA (verbatim, lesson-deck version)** [PDF L03 p22]:
  1. "Ignore this email and I'll eventually get the picture and write sad poetry about the deal we never did. (MOST POPULAR)"
  2. "Hit 'reply' instead of 'delete' and make my day, month and year. (RECOMMENDED)"
  3. "WILDCARD - Call me ... and interrupt my day like I have yours. It's ok. I deserve it. (LIMITED TIME OFFER)"

- **The funniest option goes first.** This breaks the usual "funniest third" rule on purpose [L03 22:51].
- It parodies marketers' CTA labels. For marketers, word it as "three convenient calls to action". The 8-part version is Email #6 in cold-email.md.

## 6. The Way In

- **Find the tension and joke about it.** Every message needs "The Way In". The default tension is that you're a stranger in their inbox for "my own commercial ambitions". "This potential tension provides an opportunity for humour, which not only breaks the tension but ingratiates you" [SITE 031].
- **Answer the stranger question first.** "Who are you? And what are you doing in my inbox?" Don't pretend the elephant isn't there and jump straight to the pitch.
- **The premise can be the Way In.** Example: a B2B supplier sending a Black Friday email ("Huh? Why is a [marketer] sending me...?").
- **The signature lines are optional.** His Charming Cold Open pack "used none of the devices and lines ... And yes, those templates also work" [SITE 031]. Don't stuff every move in.

## 7. Tone guardrails

**Dose**
- **The blend: funny, serious, funny, serious.** Get a laugh before each request, with "enough serious stuff, that the humour is surprising" [L03 31:06–32:07].
- **Read it aloud.** If it's just jokes with no content, cut.
- **Don't reuse a formula in one email.** One triple per email, funny words sparingly, no forced devices.
- **Context rule.** "When the context changes, so must the copy." The stranger-in-the-inbox joke assumes they have no idea who you are [NL 2023-03-17 deploying-charming-copy].
- **No fixed formula count.** "One or two formulas per message" is not a Buchan rule (legacy; no source). The blend, the read-aloud test and the one-triple limit govern instead.

**When to stop being funny**
- **After a positive reply, drop the irreverence.** Show enthusiasm and expertise instead. "Good lord – can you imagine how annoying that would be?" [SITE 031]
- **Humour opens the door; it doesn't close the deal** [SITE 024].

**Offence and who you lose**
- **Choose safe targets:** yourself, a shared enemy, or the situation. **Never mock the prospect:** "Don't mock the prospect! Self-deprecating humour is safe. However, don't be self-deprecating about your skills" [INT selzy].
- **Avoid "faux cocky".** It "can just come off as arrogance, as you don't have the benefit of intonation" [INT selzy]. Understate instead.
- **The only topic exclusion** is "when you're selling something that is life and death related" [INT selzy].
- **Where the line is:** "deliberately mentioning topics that were controversial", or going "overboard on the silliness" so you're not taken seriously [INT modern-sales-pt1 12:47].
- **Daring words are fine in context.** Unusual words are hard to take offence at.
- **References must land** with this audience (memes, pop culture, press-release format).
- **Losing the stern is a feature.** "The stern and the self-important immediately disqualify themselves" [SITE 034]. Prospects who complain about professionalism are a "BONUS".
- **Negative replies are noise unless they dominate.** "It's just words on the internet." Only worry if replies are all negative; then fix the targeting or the copy. Stopping a campaign with "an inbox full of positive responses" because of one negative is "unforgivable" [SITE 012].
- **Ignore "be more professional" critics.** "They're not wrong. They're just less right than me" [SITE 034].
- **Targeting supports the tone.** Search for profile keywords such as "humor", "funny" and "maverick".
- **Imperfect action.** "Don't be sloppy but don't be the reverse of it." Review, then ship.
- **No deception.** A fake "RE:" subject is the "antithesis of what I teach" [NL 2023-08-17 re-fake-subject-line].
- **List voice ≠ prospect voice.** His newsletter profanity ("keep that absolute piece of shit email short" [NL 2025-12-22 my-recent-talk-at-the-london-school]) is written to his own list. Never put it in cold outreach.

## 8. Readability and format

- **Succinct.** "Cut it down ... Don't blabber."
- **Rhythm.** Make it an enjoyable read; read it out loud.
- **No jargon.** Don't rehash statistics or trends.
- **Short lines with white space; no walls of text.** Stated for LinkedIn posts ("No walls of text!", one line then a space) and proposals ("Nobody likes to read walls of text!"); in cold email it is observed in all his templates rather than stated as a rule.
- **A list should reward reading to the end:** put a surprise last item.
- **Plain follow-ups can be deliberate.** One is a casual one-liner with "Sent from my iPhone" (see cold-email.md).
- **Optional:** Hemingway grade 6, fewer adverbs and less passive voice. **(unverified — legacy as a cold-email rule.)** He only shows the tool in the proposal lesson.

## Drafting checklist

1. **Unusual greeting?** "Greetings", "Bonjour", "Hey hey", "Howdy". Never "Dear… hope this finds you well".
2. **The Way In:** an amusing, honest opening line that answers "Who are you? And what are you doing in my inbox?"
3. **Funny → serious → funny.** A laugh before each request, with real content between the laughs.
4. **No formula used twice.** At most one triple; funny words sparingly; nothing forced.
5. **Understated, no hype, no jargon, no ego.** The pitch is "I help [X] without [Y]" in plain words.
6. **The ask is sugar-coated as their gain**, with an easy (ideally funny) way out.
7. **A fun ending** (pet or absurd photo, formal-assumptive closer) and a three-title signature (serious / descriptive / silly).
8. **Every joke targets you (not your skills), a shared enemy or the situation; never the prospect; no faux cocky;** the references will land with this audience, and the offer is relevant (humour won't rescue it).
9. **Every claim, compliment and fact is genuine and defensible.**
10. **Read it aloud.** Is it daring enough, does the rhythm work, and is the irreverence dropped if this replies to a warm prospect?
