# Buchan Rubric: score every draft before delivering it

Built from Buchan's own self-check and margin notes. Full citations are in `knowledge/01-voice-and-humour.md` §6–8,
`knowledge/03-cold-email-playbook.md` and `knowledge/04-linkedin-playbook.md`.

**This is NOT a generic sales QC gate.** Buchan's CTAs are deliberately low-pressure, with an easy way out.
Never "fix" a draft by adding urgency, scarcity or a hard command.

## Step 1: Hard fails (any one means rewrite, then re-score)

| # | Hard fail | Why (Buchan) |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Anything untrue: a fake compliment, an invented proof point, client, stat or shared interest | "If you think something isn't genuine, don't include it" [PDF L01 p10] |
| H2 | A sincere stock corporate opener (quoting one as an obvious parody is fine) ("I hope this email finds you well", "I'm reaching out because…", "My name is X and I work at Y") | Answer "Who are you and what are you doing in my inbox?" with honesty instead [SITE 031] |
| H3 | Sincere jargon or hype (an obvious parody of jargon is fine) ("innovative", "cutting-edge", "solution", "leverage", "synergy", "game-changer", "10x", "revolutionise", "best-in-class") | Mistake #2: "persuade with jargon" [PDF L09 p33]; "Avoid hype" [SITE 010] |
| H4 | The prospect (their looks, intelligence, company or a protected trait) is the butt of the joke | Aim jokes at yourself, the situation or a shared enemy [L03 4:18] [L03 19:46] [SITE 020] |
| H5 | A high-pressure CTA (urgency, scarcity, "book now", guilt) and no easy way out | Sugar-coat the ask; give an escape hatch [PDF L03 p21] [PDF L05 p19] [PDF 8-Part p2] |
| H6 | All jokes and no content: the reader can't say what the sender does or what's being asked | "If it just sounds like you're just trying to be funny over and over… cut" [L03 32:07] |
| H7 | **LinkedIn invite** over 300 characters (count them) | LinkedIn platform limit; Buchan: "space is limited" [L05 5:16] |
| H8 | **Reply to a positive response** that keeps up the jokey tone instead of switching to enthusiasm and expertise | "can you imagine how annoying that would be?" [SITE 031] |
| H9 | Uses a merge field you don't have, or `{placeholder}` text left unfilled in a draft marked final | Scale mode: first-name tag only [L05] |

## Step 2: Score (0–2 each; 0 = missing, 1 = present but weak, 2 = nails it, N/A = doesn't apply to this mode)

| # | Criterion | 2 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | **The Way In** | The opening deals with the awkward truth (stranger, list, pitch, no reply yet) with honesty and a smirk, or with a curiosity premise [SITE 031] |
| S2 | **Greeting / pattern interrupt** | Any opener Buchan uses: "Greetings", "Bonjour", "Hey hey", "Howdy", a compliment to the team for catch-all inboxes. In follow-ups, plain "Hey/Hi {name}", "Thanks for connecting", or **no greeting at all** (Buchan's Email #2) count as 2, because the interrupt there is the content [PDF L04 p14] [L03 29:03] [PDF 8-Part] [PDF L05 p18] |
| S3 | **Funny → serious → funny rhythm** | A laugh lands just before each request, and real content sits between the jokes [L03 31:06–32:07] |
| S4 | **Formula craft** | Formulas executed cleanly (Reverse, Triple, List, Understatement, etc.); no formula repeated in one message (a closing pair that comes straight from Buchan's template doesn't count as a repeat); funny words used sparingly; nothing forced [PDF L09 p43] [PDF L03 p15] [L03 24:57] |
| S5 | **Clear, understated pitch** | One sentence a stranger understands: who you help and with what. Understated but ambitious, meek but direct [SITE 010] |
| S6 | **Gain-framed ask** | The call or reply feels like *their* gain: "ideas you're free to steal", entertainment, trivia [SITE 034] [PDF L03 p21] |
| S7 | **Escape hatch** | An easy, ideally funny, way to say no ("send me your most cutting insult", "even if it's to tell me to politely go away") [PDF L05 p19] [PDF 8-Part p2] |
| S8 | **Fun ending / props** | Any one of: pet photo ("favourite weapon"), a formal-assumptive closer ("I await your profanity-filled response"), or a callback. The magic-email check wants both a fun ending and a closer [PDF 8-Part p1] [PDF L03 p14] |
| S9 | **Human signature** | Warm sign-off ("Have an absolutely wonderful day") plus three job titles (serious / descriptive / silly) [PDF L03 p6] |
| S10 | **Specific and genuine personalisation** | Key-prospect modes use a real detail about them or their sector; scale modes use a sector in-joke or keyword with no fake intimacy [PDF L04 p23] [PDF L05 p18] |
| S11 | **Succinct, spoken rhythm** | Short lines, white space, reads aloud like one person talking; no throat-clearing [PDF L09 p33, p43] |
| S12 | **Daring** | It would actually stand out in their inbox; it doesn't "play it safe" [PDF L09 p33] [SITE 012] |

### Which criteria apply (score only ✓; N/A means the criterion is skipped, not scored 0)

Buchan's follow-ups, nudges and invites are deliberately minimal. Judge each piece against what *its* job is, not against
the magic email.

| Mode / piece | S1 | S2 | S3 | S4 | S5 | S6 | S7 | S8 | S9 | S10 | S11 | S12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| magic-email, sequence email #1, direct-mail email | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| sequence follow-ups (#2–#7), email nudges | ✓ | ✓ | – | ✓ | – | ○ | ○ | – | – | – | ✓ | ✓ |
| breakup (#8) | ✓ | ✓ | – | – | – | – | ✓ | – | – | – | ✓ | – |
| li-invite (scale or key) | ✓ | – | – | ✓ | – | – | ○ | – | – | ○ | ✓ | ✓ |
| li-post-accept, sector/keyword follow-up | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | – | – | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| reply (positive) | – | – | – | – | ✓ | ✓ | – | – | – | ✓ | ✓ | – |
| reply (not now / negative) | – | – | – | ✓ | – | – | ✓ | – | – | – | ✓ | – |
| copy-* (sales letter, landing page, newsletter, posts, bio) | see the **Rubric map** in `copywriting.md`: "required" means ✓ and "optional" means ○ | | | | | | | | | | | |
| rewrite | as the mode the rewritten piece belongs to, with S8/S9 marked ○ (a short original needn't grow into a full magic email) | | | | | | | | | | | |

○ = score it only if the piece contains that element (e.g. a "forgot to mention" nudge with no ask skips S6/S7; a generic-human
scale invite skips S7 and S10).

**Threshold:** score ≥ **75% of the applicable maximum**. Each applicable criterion is worth 2. For example, all 12 apply to the magic email, so
24 × 0.75 = 18; a breakup has 4 applicable criteria, so it needs 6/8. No applicable criterion may score 0.

**Compliance footer** (opt-out line, postal address) is required on first-touch and sequence cold emails (see SKILL.md hard rules).
It is never scored and never counts against S11 or the word count.

**Calibration rule:** a verbatim Buchan template, filled in honestly, passes by definition. If the rubric fails one, the
rubric is wrong. Note it in the output; don't "improve" his copy to game the score. Adapted versions should be at least as good.

## Step 3: Mode-specific checks (all must pass)

- **magic-email:** greeting → honest "who I am / why you're getting this" → what I help with → low-pressure ask with gain →
  fun ending → closer → three titles [PDF Magic-Cold-Email p1] [PDF L04 p14].
- **sequence:** each email has one job and a different angle. Spacing is ≥5 working days apart, in-thread ([NL 2026-02-02]; the course said about one a week [L04 26:46] [SITE 025]).
  The breakup is polite and leaves the door open. Humour is optional: the course's 8-part breakup is plain, while
  the "funny each time" sequence ends on a funny one [PDF 8-Part p2] [PDF L04 p28–29].
- **direct-mail-combo:** the email references the physical item by name in the subject or first line ("Sorry for the ferret in the post").
- **li-invite-scale / li-invite-key:** ≤300 characters. The invite either sets up the follow-up (callback hook) or is Buchan's
  generic-but-human scale invite. Key mode references their real activity or a profile keyword.
- **li-post-accept:** if the invite carried a joke or hook, open by calling it back ("Thanks for not sending me a cutting insult!").
  Be honest that a pitch is coming, and make a soft, gain-framed ask [PDF L05 p20].
- **reply:** positive replies mean dropping the jokes and showing enthusiasm and expertise. Ask what time suits *them*, with the
  booking link as the alternative [PDF t10 p5] [L10 6:18]. Not-now replies get one light line of charm, acceptance with no pushback, and
  a dated follow-up promise. Negative replies get a polite apology and removal from the list [PDF t10 p3–4] [SITE 031].
- **rewrite:**
  - Keep the sender's real offer. If the draft's offer and the sender profile disagree, the profile wins, and you note the change.
  - You may add true facts from the sender profile and the given prospect facts. Never invent facts.
