# Cold Email Frameworks: Master Template v2

**What this is:** the house method for building a cold email frameworks doc, for any offer and any niche, plus the attack plan and checklists that make the doc launchable. v2 absorbs the Universal Teaching Edition v1 (Sep 9, 2026), the practice in Justin's newest docs (Real Experts Series v1, Oct 2, 2026; Trades AU-NZ v2; Local Business v4), Jon Buchan's techniques in plain voice, and the Oct 2026 video research (Nowoslawski, Lead Gen Jay, Hormozi via Instantly). It replaces the cloud-only "MASTER TEMPLATE (General Use) v1".

**The one-sentence philosophy:** cold email works when a real person with a true offer, sent from healthy infrastructure to the right list, says one relevant thing, presses one real pain, states the deal plainly, and asks one small question with an easy way out. Everything here forces those things and forbids everything else.

**Rules live in `copy-rules.md`.** This template says what a doc must contain and in what order. Each doc restates the rules in its own section 3, adapted to the offer, so the doc stands alone for the sending agent.

# PART 1: WHAT A FRAMEWORK IS

A **framework** is a repeatable ANGLE: a named way into the conversation, tight enough that a hundred different emails can be written from it and every one lands the same move on a different lead. It is not a reusable email.

Every framework has **eight parts** (v2 adds the spin set):

1. **Name.** Short and vivid, after its scene or move ("The Ninety Seconds", "No Pitch, Nothing Hidden"). Can't name it, the angle isn't sharp.
2. **Angle.** Two to four sentences: what the email makes them feel and realise, and why that leads to a reply.
3. **Pain pairing.** Exactly one pain from the ammo bank.
4. **Signature lines used.** Which of the offer's key phrasings it may carry, honouring each line's home.
5. **Subject options.** Three or more, lowercase, 1 to 4 words.
6. **Structure.** The four variables as a fill-in outline.
7. **Example email** plus **its follow-up**. Not to be copied; it shows the move at full quality.
8. **Spin set.** Instantly spintax for the subject, greeting and sign-off (and any stock line), each option passing every rule.

A **frameworks doc** is ten frameworks plus two or three alternates, wrapped in the attack plan, shared rules, research process, campaign structure, reply playbook and checklists, for ONE offer aimed at ONE niche. New offer or new niche = new doc.

Two unifying devices, found before anything is written:

- **The signature image:** one concrete scene from the niche's daily life where the offer's absence hurts (the Sunday night office; the mechanic who hears the timing belt before the driver gets out). Every framework echoes it in that lead's own field.
- **The spine:** the one true emotional thread in EVERY email, usually real FOMO (the conversation is happening without them, the money is already moving, someone less deserving is already trusted). If the FOMO would need inventing, the offer is not ready for cold email; send it back to the Strategist.

# PART 3: WHAT GOES IN EACH SECTION

## Header block

- **Purpose:** what the doc is (10 frameworks, follow-ups, alternates, closers, 22 touches), and the ONE goal of every email (a reply).
- **What we're offering / inviting them to**, in one plain paragraph.
- **The signature image** and **the spine**, written out.
- **Naming mode:** branded (name once, in the offer sentence, never in a subject) or unbranded (no names anywhere; first-name sign-off; the name comes out on reply).
- **Close type:** walkthrough (default) or direct ask (declared). See `copy-rules.md` §5.
- **Tracks** if the market splits (which alternate leads each, which Closer 2 variant each gets).
- **Niche, Positioning, Sender, Word count, Tone, Campaign structure** (one line each; campaign structure points at the policy section).

## §0 Attack Plan (new in v2; the Strategist writes it from `strategy-playbook.md` §11)

A one-to-two page brief at the top of the doc so anyone reading knows how we're attacking, not just what we're saying.

1. **Target:** ICP, hard filters, segments, TAM estimate and how it was estimated.
2. **Signals:** what the list is filtered on, why it makes the offer timely, how List & Signals gets it in-house.
3. **Offer score:** the 5 cold-traffic pillars, 0 to 2 each, with the fix for any weak pillar. Verdict: GO or FIX FIRST.
4. **The free thing:** what it is, why it's something people normally pay for, how it is produced from research the same day.
5. **Proof on file:** exact numbers true today, named by type. What may NOT be claimed.
6. **Campaign mode:** Standard 22-touch (default), Sprint, and whether a Spearhead tier runs. Why.
7. **Capacity math:** sends a day, inboxes, domains, insurance, new leads a day.
8. **Testing plan:** first test, variants, verdict thresholds (1,000 sends: 0 positives kill, 1 positive scale to 3,000), who reads results and where they're logged (Obsidian).
9. **Reply plan:** who answers, target response time, calendar and slot settings, caller hand-off.
10. **Trust readiness:** sender profile, LinkedIn activity, domains, photos.
11. **Recycle plan:** when non-repliers come back and with what new angle.
12. **Risks and open items.**

## §1 Facts / claims section

Only when the doc leans on news, statistics, a regulatory event or a thesis. What actually happened (verified from primary sources before writing), allowed language, banned language, freshness rule. One overstated claim the lead Googles kills every future email.

## §2 Sender roster

When several inboxes send under different names: one row per flag that changes the copy (who hosts, whose background the credibility line describes). Missing roster = all flags false.

## §3 Cold email best practices

Restate `copy-rules.md` for this offer: subject rules, the four variables, the close (with a close-variation bank of 5+ phrasings), the signature-line table (exact phrasing, home, limit), hard rules, banned words (base list + this doc's additions + the niche-vocabulary exceptions), money rules, claims rules, reading level, spintax rules, opt-out lines for this doc.

## §4 Do's and Don'ts

The general set (Part 5 below) plus doc-specific additions.

## §5 to §8 Voice, swaps, reasoning aids, niche language

As needed: rhythm, openers, sign-offs, words that sound right and wrong in the locale, spelling, caricature ban; locale swap table; partner maps or buckets the agent must reason with per lead; words they use for customers, partners, the work, and words never to use.

## §9 The 10-pain ammo bank

The niche's reality in their own language, one short paragraph per pain. Pain #1 is the signature image. Plus 2 to 3 bench pains for alternates and odd segments. One pain per email, never stacked. Sources: what they complain about publicly, what the offer fixes, what their customers and competitors do to them, what they won't say out loud.

## §10 Segment swap table

Per segment: their word for customers, the scene for pain #1, detail swaps, frameworks to skip or swap, who the real buyer is.

## §11 Research

- **Where to look**, in order (their site, Google Business Profile and 20+ reviews, LinkedIn, socials, press and awards, licenses and associations, two minutes on industry context).
- **The specificity test** with Gold, Silver, Bronze definitions and examples for this niche.
- **The 10-minute rule:** the relevance line says what a smart person would conclude after 10 minutes of looking, not "loved your website".
- **Research checklist (10 to 13 items, logged per lead with the source):** segment, first name, track, N passing details (one per framework email that needs one, never reused), the signal the list was filtered on, inputs every conditional framework needs, the free thing's inputs, regulated-field check, sender-truth check, and real local candidates from the list tool for maps (never named in email).
- **Routing rule for thin research:** what runs when too few details pass, and when to skip the lead. Never send a bracket, a guess or an invented detail.

## §12 Offer block (for the agent and setter, never pasted)

What's true: the mechanism, the terms, how fast it goes live, the reply reward, the free thing, what's behind the curtain and never mentioned, and what's NOT true and never said. Story block if a true first-person story exists for the sender (1 to 2 sentences max per email, told once per lead).

## Frameworks 1 to 10 and Alternates A to C

Fill these roles across the ten (from v1): **Signature** (pain #1, the default opener), **Terms-led** (may stack three signature lines), **Revenue home**, **Credibility home**, **Competitor** (categories only), **Free-thing** (back half), **Scarcity** (back half, only structurally true), **Soft-diagnostic** (ends on a question, no close), **Story** (only if true for the sender), and **Alternates** for segments, tracks and thin research ("when it swaps in" stated).

Each framework, in this exact shape:

```

**Angle:** ...
**Pain pairing:** #k (...)
**Signature lines used:** ...
**Subject line options:** `one` | `two` | `three`
**Structure:**

>

**Example ({segment}, {town}):**

> Hey {Name},
>

**Follow-up (reply in thread, 3 days later):**
```

Each follow-up gets a distinct job from the follow-up type map in `buchan-techniques.md` §10 (forgot to mention, the useful one, yes-or-no both welcome, three options, and so on). Ten follow-ups, ten different jobs where possible.

## Framework selection logic

Table: research finding → LEAD framework, with the default when nothing stands out. Theme buckets (never two in a row from one bucket). Default order. Track routing. Checks: scarcity and free-thing in the back half; designated lines within their limits.

## Campaign structure: the policy (Sept 3, 2026, unchanged)

1. Each framework is its own custom email.
2. There's a follow-up to it, in the same thread, written for that framework's angle.
3. The next framework is a custom email with one follow-up to it.
4. Every campaign runs all ten frameworks this way, then two more follow-ups at the end.

10 framework emails + 10 follow-ups + 2 closers = **22 touches per lead**. Framework day 0, its follow-up 3 days later, the next framework 3 days after that. About two months per lead.

| Step | What | Thread | Subject |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 3, 5 ... 19 | Framework emails, lead first from the selection table | new thread each | its own lowercase subject (spun) |
| 2, 4, 6 ... 20 | The follow-up to the framework just sent | reply in that thread | none |
| 21 | Closer 1, the short one | new thread | `quick one` (spun: `quick one` / `one question` / `still useful?`) |
| 22 | Closer 2, the breakup, with the free thing | reply in thread | none |

Rules: every framework email stands alone; follow-ups reference only their own framework; no links in framework emails, one max in follow-ups and closers; any reply pauses and hands to the reply playbook; the second-agent QC checks all 22 touches per lead before inboxes are attached; in Instantly, 22 steps, odd new threads, even replies in thread.

**Sprint mode** (declared in §0 only): touch 1 (lead framework) + its follow-up, then the booking cadence and caller from the reply playbook. QC still runs on both touches.

**The free thing** is named here, with how it is produced and when it is offered (free-thing framework and Closer 2).

## The two closers

- **Closer 1** (step 21, new thread, under 50 words): the permission question, with an opt-out built in. Shape: "I've sent a few notes about {the thing}. I'd rather ask than keep guessing. Worth a 15 minute look, or should I stop cluttering your inbox?"
- **Closer 2** (step 22, reply in thread, under 100 words): last one, no hard feelings, the terms restated plainly (the one place the full terms stack is allowed), the free thing offered with no strings ("free" may appear here, once), ends "Fair enough?". Variants per track and for thin research.

## When they reply (upgraded in v2)

Built from `reply-playbook.md`, in the doc's voice: the triage table; templates for keen, tell me more, how much, what's the catch, not now (with and without a date), who are you, not interested; the booking cadence (2 times + link → lost-times at +1 day → company-specific reason → lost-times → caller); niche-specific objections. Every link carries the lead's ref.

## Infrastructure & launch checklist (new in v2)

The Deliverability Engineer's gate from `deliverability.md` §11, filled in for this campaign (domains named, capacity numbers, warmup dates, sequencer settings), plus the pre-launch copy gate from `checklists.md`.

## Weekly rhythm & health checks (new in v2)

The five-level inbox health process from `deliverability.md` §8 with this campaign's owners and schedule, plus the Analyst's weekly read (verdicts at 1,000 sends, next test, recycle dates) logged to the Obsidian vault.

## QC checklist: the second-agent pass

12 to 16 numbered checks a second agent (never the writer) runs on all 22 touches per lead. The base set is in `checklists.md` §2; add doc-specific checks (naming, roster, designated lines, routing, regulated-field rules). Touch 1 gets the strictest read.

## Open items

Anything that must be true before launch and isn't yet (a page rename, a calendar event, roster flags, a parked line). Nothing ships with an open item that touches the copy.

# PART 4: HOW TO BUILD A DOC (the steps, in order)

1. **Strategist: the Attack Plan.** Score the offer; FIX FIRST stops the build. Define the free thing and the campaign mode.
2. **Researcher: verify every fact** to be leaned on, from primary sources. Draft §1 first.
3. **Copy Chief: signature image and spine.** Then the signature-line table (5 to 8 lines, each with a home and limit), the 10-pain ammo bank, the framework roles map and the selection logic.
4. **List & Signals: research section.** Where to look, the specificity test for this niche, the checklist, thin-research routing, the signal definitions.
5. **Framework writers (three in parallel): frameworks and alternates,** each a different pain, roles filled, examples in locale-real names and towns.
6. **Follow-up and closer writer:** ten follow-ups (distinct jobs), two closers and variants, all spin sets.
7. **Buchan editor:** honest Way In where needed, one disarm per email at most, escape hatch on every ask, opt-out lines in closers. No humour.
8. **Plain-English editor:** grade 5 target (7 ceiling), exact numbers, never mass-sent, read-aloud.
9. **Reply Setter:** "When they reply". **Deliverability Engineer:** infrastructure checklist and weekly rhythm.
10. **QC Auditor:** run `verify_doc.py`, then the QC checklist by reading. Back to the writer for any flag. Repeat until zero.
11. **Executive Director:** final read of §0 against the frameworks (does the copy attack the way the plan says?), open items listed, deliver.

# PART 5: DO'S AND DON'TS (general set; every doc adds its own)

**DO**
- Lead with what is already true without you: the conversation already happening, the money already moving.
- Make them feel seen. The relevance line should make them think "how did they know that".
- Say the terms early when in doubt. Terms answer "what's the catch" before it's asked.
- Use their words everywhere (customers, scenes, vocabulary).
- Use exact numbers for anything that already happened; "built to add" for anything forward-looking.
- Keep one detail and one pain per email. Give every ask an easy way out.
- Keep it short enough to read on a phone between two jobs.
- Mention the signal you filtered on. It's the reason you're writing.

**DON'T**
- Don't hype, flatter, or lecture them about their own business.
- Don't invent anything. No fake numbers, guests, clients or local details.
- Don't fake urgency. Only structurally true scarcity, back half only.
- Don't sound like an MLM ("opportunity", "be your own boss", "passive income").
- Don't stack pains or signature lines. Don't use dashes or exclamation marks. Don't break the naming rule.
- Don't look mass-sent. Don't write anything that would fit on a marketing page.
- Don't put prices, "free" or links in a first touch.
- Don't make jokes. Honesty does the disarming.

# PART 6: WORKED EXAMPLE (the moves in miniature)

**Offer:** we clean up and then run the books for independent restaurants; a licensed accountant reviews everything; the first cleanup is at no charge; the owner stops doing Sunday-night spreadsheets.

**Signature image:** the Sunday night office. **Spine:** every week the books stay messy, decisions get made blind while the place next door knows its numbers.

**Signature framework example (walkthrough close, easy out):**

> Hey Rosa,
>
> Saw La Cocina's been family-run on 5th for twelve years.
>
> it's Sunday night somewhere in your week. Service is over, everyone went home, and you're alone with the laptop and a stack of receipts. Nobody opens a restaurant to do that part.
>
> That's the part we take. We clean up last quarter first, at no charge. A licensed accountant checks it. After that the books just show up done.
>
> Worth a look? Reply "books" and I'll send the rundown. Yours either way.
>
> Dana

**Its follow-up (forgot-to-mention job, one question):**

> Hey Rosa, one thing I left out of the Sunday night note. You see every number before we file anything, nothing goes out without you. Fifteen minutes here if it's worth it: {calendar link}. What would you do with your Sundays back?

**Spin set:**
    subject: {{RANDOM | sunday night | the receipts | after service}}
    greeting: {{RANDOM | Hey | Hi}} Rosa,
    sign-off: {{RANDOM | Dana | Thanks, Dana | Cheers, Dana}}

**Deriving a framework from one pain (the smallest unit of the craft):** take any pain and ask (1) who gets paid or trusted instead of the lead (the FOMO), (2) what scene makes it concrete (the dressing), (3) which signature line answers it (the offer sentence), (4) what would they reply to get (the close). Run it ten times against ten true pains, inside the rules. The rules keep it honest. The research makes it personal. The structure makes it relentless without being rude.
