# Copy Rules: the one rulebook

Every framework email, follow-up, closer and reply written under this skill follows these rules. Where an older skill disagrees, this file wins (see `conflict-resolutions.md` for why). Source tags: [HOUSE] Justin's frameworks docs, [BUCHAN] Jon Buchan, [ERIC] Eric Nowoslawski, [HORMOZI] Alex Hormozi, [INSTANTLY] the Instantly narrator, [JAY] Lead Gen Jay.

---

## 1. The job of each piece

- **Subject:** get the open. Nothing else. [HOUSE]
- **Framework email:** get a reply. Not a booking, not a click. The reply unlocks the reward, the page and the setter. [HOUSE] [BUCHAN: "the job of the cold email is to sell the idea that a call is not a terrible idea"]
- **Follow-up:** remind them you exist and add one new reason to answer. Never nag. [BUCHAN]
- **Closer 1:** ask permission to continue. **Closer 2:** end cleanly, restate the terms, leave the free thing.
- **Touch 1 matters most.** About 99% of positive replies come from the first email in most campaigns [ERIC]. QC touch 1 hardest. Keep the 22-touch sequence anyway; it catches the distracted ones [BUCHAN reaction type 3].

## 2. Length and reading level

| Piece | Words (greeting + sign-off counted) |
|---|---|
| Framework email | 60 to 100, aim 80 to 95 |
| Follow-up | 30 to 50, ends on exactly one question |
| Closer 1 | under 50 |
| Closer 2 (breakup) | under 100 |
| Reply templates | as short as the question allows; usually under 80 |

- **Reading level: grade 5 or lower is the target. Grade 7 is the ceiling.** Hormozi rewrote his templates below a 3rd-grade level and response rose 50% [HORMOZI]. Short words, short sentences, one idea per sentence. The verifier computes Flesch-Kincaid grade per email.
- **Half a page, max.** If they have to scroll, you lost them. [HORMOZI] [INSTANTLY]
- **Phone test:** readable on a phone between two jobs, no scrolling. [HOUSE]

## 3. Subject lines

- 1 to 4 words. Lowercase. Vague. Looks like a peer or customer sent it. Never selling, never the brand name. [HOUSE]
- Every framework gets **3 or more subject variants** written as Instantly spintax in its spin set. Eric ran about 20 variants on one subject slot. [ERIC]
- No fake "re:" or "fwd:". It is deception. [BUCHAN]
- Banned in subjects: "free", "quick question", "touching base", any number with a % or $.
- **Optional test, flagged:** the competitor open-loop subject (the prospect's nearest competitor *category or local name* as the subject). This is the Instantly narrator's adaptation of a Hormozi voicemail play, untested in data. Only run it as a declared A/B test, and only where naming a competitor in the subject does not break the doc's naming rule.

## 4. Body structure (four variables, in order) [HOUSE]

1. **Relevance:** one sentence, in the first two lines, carrying the ONE research detail. It must pass the specificity test (section 6). It may also answer the stranger question honestly (Buchan's Way In, in plain voice; see `buchan-techniques.md`).
2. **Pain:** ONE pain from the ammo bank, in their words. Never stack two.
3. **Offer:** the mechanism stated plainly, carrying two or three signature lines at most.
4. **Close:** one small ask, reply-triggered, with an easy way out.

## 5. The close

- **Default: the walkthrough close.** One decision in the email: reply. The reward is named and immediate (one-pager, rundown, map, three questions, the page). Then "15 minutes". Then "yours either way". [HOUSE]
- **Declared alternative: the direct ask.** A doc may declare in its header that framework emails end on a direct number or call ask (the referral and trades pattern). If declared, every email in that doc follows it. Never mix silently.
- At least 8 of 10 frameworks use the doc's declared close; 1 or 2 may end on a soft diagnostic question.
- **Easy out, every close.** Pair the ask with a way out ("if it's not for you, no problem"). [BUCHAN escape hatch]
- **Word it fresh.** No two emails to one lead close with the same sentence. Keep a close-variation bank in the doc. [HOUSE]
- Never "book a call with me", "hop on a quick call", "I'd love to".
- The reward is something they would otherwise pay for, not a Loom, a PDF case study or "a call". [HORMOZI lead-magnet rule]

## 6. Research and personalisation

- **No email before research.** Exactly one true, specific detail per email, never reused across a lead's 22 touches. [HOUSE]
- **The specificity test:** could this sentence go to any other business in their category? If yes, it fails. Gold = their own words or a skill named in reviews. Silver = a specific fact about them. Bronze = city plus category, which never counts alone. [HOUSE, Real Experts doc]
- **The 10-minute rule:** write what a smart person would conclude after 10 minutes of looking at this business, not "I loved your website" or "I see you've been CEO for 2 years". [ERIC]
- **Three honest types of personal line:** Opportunity (something you spotted they could improve), Commonality (real shared ground), Topicality (news, a launch, a post). Never fake common ground. [BUCHAN]
- **Signal first.** The strongest relevance line is a signal you filtered the list on (hiring, a new location, running ads, a review pattern, a job change in the last 90 days). Mention it, because it is the reason you are writing. [HORMOZI] [ERIC] [BUCHAN]
- **One detail, not three.** One proves you looked. Three reads like surveillance. [HOUSE] [BUCHAN]
- Thin research routes to the thin-research alternate. Too thin to pass: skip the lead. Never send a bracket, a guess or an invented detail.

## 7. Never look mass-sent [HORMOZI]

- "The best way to get someone to ignore one-to-one communication is to make it look like one-to-many."
- Not too formal. Not weirdly personalised. Read it out loud: does it sound like one person wrote it to one person?
- Spintax the static parts (greeting, sign-off, closer stock lines) at word level so no two sends are byte-identical (section 12).

## 8. Numbers and claims

- **Exact numbers, never ranges, for anything that already happened.** "Our average gym signs up 17.3 members in the first 30 days", not "20 to 30". Ranges read as marketing. [HORMOZI]
- **Forward-looking claims are never precise promises.** The revenue line stays "built to add {honest range}" or "the target is", once per email, only in its designated frameworks. [HOUSE]
- **Never fabricate.** No invented stats, results, case studies, partner names, guests or local details. "Another client" only when a real, named result is on file. Directional truths only ("most owners I talk to"). [HOUSE] [BUCHAN "genuine only"]
- **Look-alike proof wins.** "We did X for a company that looks just like you" is the strongest proof, when true. [ERIC]
- **Claims rule:** never promise outcomes. Where a licensed party decides (provider, lender, attorney, insurer), say so. Competitor categories only, never named in the body. [HOUSE]
- **News hooks and stats are verified from primary sources before writing** and carried in the doc's facts section with allowed language, banned language and a freshness rule. [HOUSE]

## 9. Money

- **No prices in cold email, ever.** No fee, no "one-off", no pricing language. [HOUSE]
- The only money lines: the revenue line where allowed, and the free thing.
- **"Free":** never in a subject, never in touch 1. Prefer "at no charge" or "yours either way". The word "free" may appear once, in Closer 2. [CONFLICT RESOLUTION 7]
- No "no cost", no "risk-free", no "guarantee".

## 10. Voice (plain house voice with Buchan's techniques, no humour)

- First person "I". Peer to peer. Voicemail, not keynote. [HOUSE]
- Write to the person, not the title. Understated but ambitious. Meek but direct. [BUCHAN]
- Disarming honesty: say the awkward true thing first (this is a cold email, here is why I'm writing). [BUCHAN, plain form]
- **No jokes, no humour formulas, no pet photos, no silly job titles, no "Bonjour".** Justin's call, 2026-10-04. Buchan's techniques survive; his humour does not.
- Never mock the prospect. Never "faux cocky". Never lecture them about their own business. [BUCHAN] [HOUSE]
- After a positive reply: plain, warm, competent. Answer exactly what they asked.

## 11. Format and mechanics

- Plain text. No HTML, bold, buttons. [HOUSE]
- **Images:** off by default. An image is a test, not a ban: one campaign got fewer opens but more replies with an image [ERIC]. Only as a declared A/B test on a mature campaign.
- **Links:** none in framework emails. At most one in a follow-up or closer (calendar or the page, with the lead's ref). [HOUSE]
- **No em dashes or en dashes anywhere, subjects included.** Periods, commas or parentheses. [HOUSE]
- **No exclamation marks.** [HOUSE]
- **No parallel three-item lists.** Vary sentence length hard: long, short, fragment. [HOUSE]
- Greeting: `Hey {first_name},` by default, with locale swaps (`Hi`, `G'day` only where native). Spun at word level. Never "Dear", never "I hope this finds you well". [HOUSE]
- Sign-off: first name only (unbranded) or name plus one plain line (branded). No three-title gags.
- Every framework email stands alone. Never "as I mentioned", "my last note", "following up" inside a framework email. A follow-up may reference only its own framework's email. [HOUSE]

## 12. Spintax [ERIC]

- One campaign went from 0.3% to 1.2% reply rate the day word-level spintax was added. Content fingerprinting is real.
- **Format:** Instantly syntax, `{{RANDOM | option one | option two | option three}}`.
- **Where:** subject (3+ options), greeting, the sign-off, stock lines in follow-ups and closers, the opt-out line. Per-lead researched sentences are already unique and are not spun.
- **Word level, not sentence level.** Tell writers "do word-level spintax": swap synonyms across the sentence, not one word in a repeated sentence.
- Every option must pass every rule on its own (length, banned words, dashes, grade). The verifier renders option 1 for counting; the QC auditor reads all options.
- **Placeholders:** single braces (`{first_name}`, `{sender}`, `{page link}`) are instructions for the writing agent inside the doc. They must never reach a sent email. Spintax double braces are the only braces allowed in upload-ready copy.

## 13. Opt-out and compliance

- Every send has an unsubscribe mechanism: the sequencer's unsubscribe header or link is on for every step (pre-launch check).
- **US recipients:** a valid postal address must be present (sequencer footer or signature). CAN-SPAM. **UK/EU:** a legitimate B2B reason to write and an easy out. [BUCHAN skill hard rule]
- Plain-voice opt-out lines live in Closer 1, Closer 2 and, optionally, follow-ups. Examples: "If this isn't for you, just say so and I'll stop." / "Not your thing? One word back and you won't hear from me again."
- Never "silence = no" ("if I don't hear back I'll assume..."). Never threaten to keep emailing.
- Honest sender identity: real name on the inbox, a real person behind it. [JAY trust rule] [BUCHAN]

## 14. Banned words (body and subject)

price, cost, investment, spend, budget, unlock, 10x, synergy, leverage, innovative, cutting-edge, solution, game-changer, transform, revolutionise, revolutionize, scale your, disrupt, guaranteed, guarantee, ROI, lead generation, AI, automation, opportunity, passive income, furthermore, moreover, delve, landscape, robust, awesome, amazing, excited, honored, honoured, reach out, touch base, hope this finds, I wanted to reach out, I'd love to, hop on, quick call, risk-free, no cost, act now, limited time.

- "AI" is allowed only where the doc's claims section explicitly permits it (e.g. a thesis line), once per email.
- "Guarantee" is allowed only as a real, defined offer term the doc declares (e.g. "revenue guarantee" in Local Business v4), never as "guaranteed results".
- Doc-level exceptions are declared to the verifier with an HTML comment: `<!-- verify-allow: ai, guarantee -->`; doc-level additions with `<!-- verify-ban: podcast, exposure -->`.
- "Leads" only when naming what they already buy. "Agency" only as what you are not. The niche's own working vocabulary is allowed even when it brushes this list; the doc lists those exceptions.
- Each doc extends this list with its own facts-section bans and naming blocklist.
- Locale: never American terms in AU, NZ or UK docs (realtor, HVAC, contractor as a generic). Locale spelling throughout.

## 15. Urgency and scarcity

- No fake urgency. No countdowns, "spots left", "this month only", "act now". [HOUSE] [BUCHAN: never fix a draft by adding urgency]
- Only structurally true scarcity (there is only one first in a town; he records two interviews a day). It lives in the scarcity framework, back half only.
- Curiosity and real FOMO (the spine) do the work urgency pretends to.

## 16. The read-aloud test (last check on every piece)

Read it out loud. If it sounds like a keynote, a brochure or a robot, rewrite it until it sounds like a voicemail from a busy person who actually looked them up.
